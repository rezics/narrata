# Academic & Theoretical Models of Computational Narrative Representation (evidence for Narrata's abstract core)

Compiled 2026-10-04. Legend: **[read]** = full text read this session; **[abstract]** = abstract or landing-page read; **[secondhand]** = known only through a search snippet, a secondary summary, or another paper's description of it. Every claim below is paraphrased unless explicitly quoted. Each model is checked against four axes: **(A) core abstraction**, **(B) logic/text separation**, **(C) long/open-ended scale**, **(D) dynamic/incremental updates**.

## Q1. Narratology foundations and the computational story/discourse split: does the evidence support separating the "narrative engine" from text/media?

### Takeaway
Mostly yes. Narratology and computational narrative both converge on a **three-layer model**: (1) fabula/plot-world (what happens in the storyworld), (2) discourse/syuzhet (which events get told, in what order, and to whom), and (3) narration/presentation (how a medium realizes it). Computational systems mostly use this as a pipeline. The caveat is that the field's own 2024 survey shows the medium limits what plot can be told. Some planners deliberately break the pipeline (PLOTSHOT, BIPOCL) so that discourse constraints can feed back into story. The evidence therefore supports keeping **storyworld logic independent of the medium**. It does **not** support treating discourse (selection, order, reveal, focalization) as a rendering detail. Discourse is a separate logical layer, and it needs a two-way interface with the realizer.

### Cited Findings
- The Narrative Ontology (NOnt) uses the Russian formalists' two levels: fabula (events in chronological order) and syuzhet/plot (the way events are presented). It adds Bal's third level, *presentation*: the concrete representation delivered to the audience, such as the text of a novel. [read] — [Meghini, Bartalesi & Metilli 2021, "Representing narratives in digital libraries: The narrative ontology", *Semantic Web* 12(2):241–264](https://www.semantic-web-journal.net/system/files/swj2539.pdf); [ACM DL record](https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.3233/SW-200421)
- NOnt formally defines a narrative as three parts. The **fabula** is the story as it happened, in reality or in fiction. The **narrations** are one or more expressions, each in its own language and medium, corresponding to Bal's presentation. The **reference** relation maps fragments of narrations to fragments of the fabula, and the plot/syuzhet is *derived* from it. One fabula can have many narrations, and two narrations that are logically incompatible are, by definition, not narrations of the same fabula. [read] — [Meghini et al. 2021](https://www.semantic-web-journal.net/system/files/swj2539.pdf)
- NOnt observes that current digital libraries hold only the narration level (the objects that carry narratives) and have no representation of the fabula. The paper's aim is to make the fabula a first-class object. [read] — [Meghini et al. 2021](https://www.semantic-web-journal.net/system/files/swj2539.pdf)
- The 2024 ICAPS survey of narrative planning uses three layers: **plot** (the narrative's virtual world, made of events that are causally and purposively related changes in states-of-affairs), **discourse** (a time-ordered, intentionally selected subset of the plot, after Genette 1980), and **narration** (surface realization in a medium). It reports that formalizing all three is "often not practically feasible", so researchers choose which layers to model. [read] — [Cardona-Rivera, Jhala, Porteous & Young 2024, "The Story So Far on Narrative Planning", ICAPS 2024, pp. 489–499](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICAPS/article/download/31509/33669)
- The same survey argues the choice of narration is not just aesthetic: the medium constrains which plot information can be narrated (citing Elliott 2004 on literary film adaptation). Its example is a comic panel that needs extra dialogue to explain a ship maneuver the film could simply show. [read] — [Cardona-Rivera et al. 2024](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICAPS/article/download/31509/33669)
- Most narrative planners tacitly follow the Reiter & Dale NLG pipeline: plot is content determination, discourse is content structuring, and narration is linguistic realization. Plot planners usually give narration only a perfunctory treatment so that language effects do not confound evaluation. [read] — [Cardona-Rivera et al. 2024](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICAPS/article/download/31509/33669)
- In narrative *discourse* planning, the finished plot plan is treated as a fixed knowledge base whose elements become part of the problem language. The planner works in **audience-belief space**: communicative actions change what the audience believes. Unlike general discourse planning, audience beliefs are not assumed to grow monotonically, because belief revision is a key source of narrative effects. Examples are MISER (staging and attention) and INFER (eliciting audience inference). [read] — [Cardona-Rivera et al. 2024](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICAPS/article/download/31509/33669)
- Narration planning is under-explored because discourse planners are often tightly coupled to a specific medium. FIREBOLT, a cinematic realizer, is the noted exception. A separate line of work deliberately abandons the pipeline. PLOTSHOT derives plot-level landmarks from required discourse "snapshots", and BIPOCL co-evolves plot and discourse, on the argument that form cannot be separated from content. [read] — [Cardona-Rivera et al. 2024](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICAPS/article/download/31509/33669); BIPOCL primary: [Winer & Young 2016, "Discourse-Driven Narrative Generation With Bipartite Planning", INLG](https://aclanthology.org/W16-6602.pdf) [abstract]
- Darshak (Jhala & Young 2010) uses hierarchical POCL planning to connect cinematic discourse with narration. The effects of a compound action carry discourse-level information about the plot being filmed, and a decomposition method encodes a cinematic idiom as a sub-plan of camera shots. [read via survey] — [Cardona-Rivera et al. 2024](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICAPS/article/download/31509/33669); primary: [Jhala & Young 2010, "Cinematic Visual Discourse: Representation, Generation, and Evaluation", IEEE TCIAIG 2(2)](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/jhala2010cinematic.pdf)
- Mimesis was one of the first 3D virtual environments connected to a *separate* experience manager that used a narrative planner to adapt the story in real time. [secondhand] — [IDSwiki: Mimesis](https://tecfalabs.unige.ch/mediawiki-narrative/index.php/Mimesis)
- Chatman separates story time (the duration of the purported events) from discourse time (the time it takes to read the discourse) and, by extension, story space from discourse space. In Marie-Laure Ryan's handbook entry, the storyworld is the story space completed by the reader's imagination from cultural knowledge and real-world experience. [read] — [Ryan, "Space", *the living handbook of narratology* (2012, rev. 2014)](https://www-archiv.fdm.uni-hamburg.de/lhn/node/55.html); Chatman's book: [Chatman 1978, *Story and Discourse*](https://ia902900.us.archive.org/4/items/StoryAndDiscourseNarrativeStructureInFictionAndFilm/chatman.seymour_story.and.discourse_narrative.structure.in.fiction.and.film1.pdf) (not read)
- Koenitz's System–Process–Product (SPP) model for interactive digital narrative has three parts. The *system* holds a **protostory**, meaning the space of all potential narratives. An interactor instantiates it through a *process*, and the result is a realized *product*. Koenitz calls the structure inside the protostory the "narrative design". [secondhand] — [Koenitz 2015, "Towards a Specific Theory of Interactive Digital Narrative"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/310479591_Towards_a_Specific_Theory_of_Interactive_Digital_Narrative)
- Kybartas & Bidarra classify story-generation systems along two components, **plot** and **space**, by how much of each is automated. They argue for a *standard model of narrative* so that plot-automation and space-automation techniques can work together. [abstract] — [Kybartas & Bidarra 2017, IEEE TCIAIG 9(3):239–253](https://repository.tudelft.nl/record/uuid:0e967ade-b728-4c3f-b3bd-5b89a35b8e2b)

### Inferences
- The literature supports Narrata's thesis at the **fabula level**: storyworld state, events and causal/temporal relations can and should be independent of the medium. NOnt even writes this into its *definition*: one fabula, many narrations.
- The literature does **not** support collapsing discourse into "the renderer". Choosing which events are told, in what order, what is hidden or revealed, and what the audience is supposed to believe is logic with its own state, namely an audience/reader model. If the core models only fabula, reveal, suspense, flashback and "what the player knows" logic will leak into text scripts. Recommendation: the core should host a **discourse layer** (telling/reveal state and per-viewer knowledge) separate from both the world layer and media realizers.
- The interface to realizers should be **two-way**: realizers or media packages declare capabilities and constraints (what can be shown, how long things take), and discourse logic can query them. This is the PLOTSHOT/BIPOCL lesson.
- Koenitz's protostory/process/product maps onto Narrata as: authored node packages = protostory; deterministic runtime plus player input = process; a save/timeline branch = product. This is useful vocabulary for the time-travel store.

### Gaps
- Primary texts by Genette, Bal and Herman (storyworld) were not read; their positions here come from NOnt, Cardona-Rivera et al. and Ryan's handbook entry.
- I did not read Young's original Mimesis architecture paper, so I could not verify how its story/discourse split was implemented.
- I found no empirical study that measures the cost or benefit of a strict story/discourse/narration split in a shipped interactive product.

## Q2. Graph-based models (plot graphs, branching patterns, hypertext, Petri nets, statecharts, behavior trees, Façade's beats and drama manager): what do they imply?

### Takeaway
Graphs are everywhere, as authoring artifacts, as successor relations, and as formal control structures (Petri nets, statecharts, behavior trees). The production record says pure branching graphs do not scale (combinatorial explosion). Every practical branching pattern that re-merges depends on **state tracking**. In the more capable systems (Façade, Versu, SceneMaker) the graph or statechart is a **local control-flow device** inside a larger state-based selection system, not the core model of the story. Statecharts specifically are evidenced as a good way to separate dialogue content from control logic.

### Cited Findings
- **Ashwell's choice-based patterns (2015).** A *time cave* branches heavily with little re-merging and so needs almost no state tracking. A *gauntlet* is a linear spine with pruned branches and relies little on state. *Branch-and-bottleneck* re-merges at common events and almost always relies heavily on state tracking. *Quest* (organized by geography) and *open map* need moderate to extensive state tracking. *Sorting hat* uses state mainly early. *Floating modules* have no central plot and are gated by state or randomness, so authors can assume little about prior events. *Loop-and-grow* and *spoke-and-hub* unlock new options on each cycle through state. [read] — [Ashwell 2015, "Standard Patterns in Choice-Based Games"](https://heterogenoustasks.wordpress.com/2015/01/26/standard-patterns-in-choice-based-games/)
- **Façade's anti-branching strategy.** To avoid the combinatorial explosion of complex causal event chains, Façade splits the narrative into multiple, mostly causally independent "fronts of progression" and runs several narrative sequencers in parallel that interleave their performances. [read] — [Mateas & Stern 2005, "Structuring Content in the Façade Interactive Drama Architecture", AIIDE 2005](https://users.soe.ucsc.edu/~michaelm/publications/mateas-aiide2005.pdf)
- **Façade's beats and drama manager.** A beat contains 10–100 joint dialog behaviors (jdbs) written in ABL. Each jdb is a 1–5 line exchange implemented in 40–200 lines of ABL. Beats are annotated with preconditions, weights, priorities and story-value effects. The beat sequencer picks the unused beat whose preconditions hold and whose tension effect best matches an author-specified Aristotelian tension arc. Story state consists of social-game "scores" (affinity, hot-button tiers, therapy counters) plus overall tension, and jdbs update these values. [read] — [Mateas & Stern 2005](https://users.soe.ucsc.edu/~michaelm/publications/mateas-aiide2005.pdf)
- **Façade factors logic out of content.** Within a beat, *handler* meta-behaviors listen for interpreted player discourse acts and add, remove or reorder future beat goals, or insert "mix-ins". The authors say this factoring spared them from writing the sequencing logic into the dialogue behaviors themselves. [read] — [Mateas & Stern 2005](https://users.soe.ucsc.edu/~michaelm/publications/mateas-aiide2005.pdf)
- **Façade scale data.** A ~20-minute experience needed ~2,500 jdbs organized into 27 beats, of which ~15 appear in one playthrough. One playthrough shows at most ~25% of the content. Writing alone took ~3 person-years. Although 27 beats allow thousands of sequences, most beats are causally independent, so few sequences differ meaningfully. Global agency was lower than intended because authoring was slow. [read] — [Mateas & Stern 2005](https://users.soe.ucsc.edu/~michaelm/publications/mateas-aiide2005.pdf)
- **Search-based drama management (Weyhrauch 1997).** The story is modeled as a set of *plot points* plus an author-specified evaluation function over plot-point sequences. A drama manager searches over its own possible actions (hints, world reconfigurations) and projected player moves, and picks the action that maximizes expected story quality. [secondhand] — [Nelson & Mateas 2005, "Search-Based Drama Management in the Interactive Fiction Anchorhead", AIIDE](https://eis.ucsc.edu/papers/Nelson_Mateas_-_SBDM_-_AIIDE05.pdf); follow-up: [Nelson, Roberts, Isbell & Mateas 2006, "Reinforcement Learning for Declarative Optimization-Based Drama Management", AAMAS](https://www.kmjn.org/publications/DODM_AAMAS06.pdf)
- **Plot graphs (Scheherazade).** Scheherazade learns a plot graph for a topic from crowdsourced example stories. The graph holds events, precedence relations and mutual-exclusion relations, and the system generates stories by sampling paths that respect them. Its stories for previously unseen topics were rated comparable to simple stories by untrained humans. [abstract] — [Li, Lee-Urban, Johnston & Riedl 2013, "Story Generation with Crowdsourced Plot Graphs", AAAI](https://faculty.cc.gatech.edu/~riedl/pubs/aaai13.pdf)
- **Petri nets.** Brom & Abonyi used Petri nets that modify the reactive plans of individual actors to author non-linear plots. They report that the approach supports iterative design and copes with large worlds holding tens of actors, and it was used in the story manager of the serious game *Europe 2045*. [secondhand] — [Brom & Abonyi 2006, "Petri Nets for Game Plot" (AISB'06)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/247168849_Petri_Nets_for_Game_Plot); [IVE drama manager paper](https://artemis.ms.mff.cuni.cz/main/papers/IVE-dramamanager-2006.pdf)
- **Statecharts (Visual SceneMaker).** SceneMaker's central authoring principle is **separating dialogue content from logic**. Content lives in a *scenescript* that resembles a movie script, with utterances and stage directions. Control flow lives in a *sceneflow*, a hierarchical, concurrent statechart with variable scoping, interruption policies and runtime history, which supports resuming interrupted dialogue coherently. [abstract] — [Gebhard, Mehlmann & Kipp 2012, "Visual SceneMaker—a tool for authoring interactive virtual characters", *J. Multimodal User Interfaces*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12193-011-0077-1); [DFKI PDF](https://www.dfki.de/fileadmin/user_upload/import/6185_fulltext.pdf)
- **Behavior trees.** Parameterized behavior trees (PBTs) let trees carry data and coordinate several characters inside one reusable structure called an *event*. They were the basis for event-centric adaptive storytelling platforms such as PAStE (Shoulson, Kapadia, Badler). [secondhand] — [ADAPT / Shoulson et al. (UPenn)](https://repository.upenn.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/0d4685b9-fed9-4994-8eab-a087ad5463b0/content)
- **Versu on finite-state machines.** Versu's authors say a social practice is strictly more powerful than an FSM. A practice can store arbitrary persistent data, while an FSM remembers only its current state. A practice's actions can add any sentence to the world database, while an FSM action can only change state. Versu also describes CYOA as a static, pre-authored story graph that acts as a non-reactive drama manager. [read] — [Evans & Short, "The AI Architecture of Versu" (Versu: A Simulationist Storytelling System, IEEE TCIAIG 2014)](https://versu.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/05/versu.pdf)
- **Graph and variable models in broadcast interactive media.** ShapeShifting TV's Narrative Structure Language (NSL) is logic-based. It has one primitive, the *atomic narrative object* (a logical descriptor of a media clip), and three structures: *link*, *layer* and *selection*. [abstract] — [Ursu et al. 2008, "ShapeShifting TV: Interactive screen media narratives", *Multimedia Systems* 14(2)](https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1007/s00530-008-0119-z). BBC R&D's StoryFormer is a graph whose nodes are narrative objects, with playout controlled by variables that are preset or changed by interaction. An interactive episode of *Click* reportedly offered ~3 hours of video viewable in over 84 trillion ways. [secondhand] — [DVB Study Mission Report on Object-Based Media, 2025](https://dvb.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/S102_Study-Mission-Report-on-Object-Based-Media_May-2025-1.pdf)

### Inferences
| Model | (A) Core abstraction | (B) Logic/text separation | (C) Long/open-ended scale | (D) Dynamic updates |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Branching graph / CYOA (time cave) | Graph *is* the state | Text sits on nodes, so it is intertwined | Poor: explosion, little re-merge | Poor: new branches need edits to existing nodes |
| Branch-and-bottleneck / quest / open map | Graph + tracked variables | Text on nodes; state in variables | Moderate: re-merging needs heavy state | Moderate |
| Façade beats + drama manager | Pool of preconditioned units + numeric story state + tension-arc objective | Sequencing logic factored out of dialogue (handlers); dialogue variants keyed by state (affinity, tension) | Content-bound: 2,500 jdbs for 20 minutes | Beats can be added to the pool; jdbs are costly |
| SBDM/DODM plot points | Plot-point set + evaluation function + DM actions | Fully abstract; text is outside | Search cost grows with plot points | Evaluation function must be re-tuned |
| Plot graph (Scheherazade) | Events + precedence + mutex | Abstract events; text is realized separately | Small-topic scale | Learnable from new data |
| Petri nets | Places/tokens = world state; transitions = events | Abstract | Claimed to handle large worlds (secondhand) | Modular sub-nets |
| Statecharts (SceneMaker) | Hierarchical, concurrent control flow + history | **Explicitly separate** (scenescript vs sceneflow) | Local scale (scenes and dialogue) | Scenes swappable |
| Behavior trees / ABL | Reactive control per agent or event | Behaviors embed lines | Local scale | Reusable sub-trees |
| OBM / NSL / StoryFormer | Recursive narrative objects + conditional links + variables | **Explicitly separate** (objects reference media) | Large combinatorial playout from fixed footage | Re-edit without re-coding |

- Pattern: graphs and statecharts work well as **scoped control flow** (a scene, a conversation, a beat, a practice's internal states). They do not work as the *global* story representation. Narrata's flow VM and statecharts fit naturally as the *inside* of content units, with global sequencing done by state-based selection (Q3).
- Statechart history and concurrency (SceneMaker) and Façade's interruption/resume mechanics (gist points, re-establish lines) are runtime needs that a narrative VM should support natively.

### Gaps
- I did not obtain primary material on hypertext story graphs (Bernstein's "Patterns of Hypertext" 1998, Storyspace, Tinderbox), so I cannot report their structural claims.
- The Petri-net and PBT claims are secondhand; no scalability measurements were found.
- Weyhrauch's 1997 dissertation was not read directly.

## Q3. Non-graph, state-based models (storylets/QBN, salience, rule databases, social simulation, emergent narrative, story sifting): which scale to open-ended or very long narratives, and why?

### Takeaway
The representations that reach long, open-ended and incrementally extended narratives are **state-centric**. They pair a world-state store with a **database of independent content units** gated by predicates over that state (storylets/QBN, salience, Valve-style rules), or with **simulation plus curation over an event log** (Versu, Comme il Faut/Prom Week/Ensemble, emergent narrative, Felt/Winnow, Dwarf Fortress). They scale because content units are independent and reorderable, so adding a unit does not mean editing others. The recurring costs are **state design** (choosing which variables carry meaning), **pacing and arc control**, and **tuning** of selection or utility.

### Cited Findings
- **Storylet definition.** A storylet is a discrete, self-contained, reorderable unit of narrative content gated by preconditions. The term originated in Fallen London, where storylets were gated by numerical *qualities*. A story is assembled during play from a database of such units, with both player and system influencing which storylets come next. [read] — [Kreminski & Wardrip-Fruin 2018, "Sketching a Map of the Storylets Design Space", ICIDS 2018, LNCS 11318, pp. 160–164](https://mkremins.github.io/publications/Storylets_SketchingAMap.pdf)
- **Storylet design space.** Kreminski & Wardrip-Fruin identify four independent dimensions:
  - precondition type
  - repeatability
  - internal structure: static text, templated text, replacement grammar, or internal branching
  - content-selection architecture: player choice, random, weighted random, salience-based best match to an "ideal" state with generic fallbacks, pathfinding against a "story spec" (StoryAssembler), or a full drama manager (Façade, classified as *recursive* storylets with their own content pools)

  "Content selection architecture" is Michael Mateas's term. [read] — [Kreminski & Wardrip-Fruin 2018](https://mkremins.github.io/publications/Storylets_SketchingAMap.pdf)
- **Parametrized storylets.** In Starfreighter, preconditions are dynamic queries that **treat game state as a database**: they find characters or items that match criteria and bind them to named parameters. The chosen bindings change both the surface text and the effects on state. [read] — [Kreminski & Wardrip-Fruin 2018](https://mkremins.github.io/publications/Storylets_SketchingAMap.pdf)
- **Short's taxonomy (2016).** In quality-based narrative (QBN), the qualities *are* the world state (location included). This contrasts with salience systems that sit on an independently modeled world. QBN strengths: short stories slot together, callbacks are easy, and unordered information-gathering works without repetition. QBN weaknesses: content is dull until there are enough storylets, progression bookkeeping is manual, and the list of available storylets can become overwhelming. Salience systems are easy to start with broad defaults, but a player can accidentally satisfy all the preconditions for an unintended outcome. *Waypoint* narrative (Glass) pathfinds between topics to steer chaotic input back toward the next authored beat. Short notes that adding content there increases efficiency. [read, via fetched summary] — [Short 2016, "Beyond Branching: Quality-Based, Salience-Based, and Waypoint Narrative Structures"](https://emshort.blog/2016/04/12/beyond-branching-quality-based-and-salience-based-narrative-structures/)
- **Storylets and late content (Short 2019).** A storylet is content (dialogue, narration, animation or film) plus prerequisites plus effects on world state. Storylets support **adding content after release** without rewriting existing material, and they avoid the per-path flags that time caves need. Short recommends hybrids: storylets with internal branching, and storylets placed inside scaffolds such as gauntlet, branch-and-bottleneck or sorting hat. [read, via fetched summary] — [Short 2019, "Storylets: You Want Them"](https://emshort.blog/2019/11/29/storylets-you-want-them/)
- **Kennedy's revision of QBN (2017).** Alexis Kennedy, who coined "quality-based narrative", dropped the term. In Failbetter's design, every quality was the same kind of thing: character stats, currencies and story-progress flags. He made that choice in 2009 and later concluded it erased useful distinctions, so later games needed workarounds. He now prefers "resource narrative": resources whose nature and interrelation match the grain of the story, and which are ideally scarce, reproducible and fungible, so that events seem to arise from combinations of resource states. [read, via fetched summary] — [Kennedy 2017, "I've stopped talking about quality-based narrative, I've started talking about resource narrative"](https://weatherfactory.biz/qbn-to-resource-narratives/)
- **Valve's rule database (Ruskin, GDC 2012).** Hundreds of world facts are fuzzy-matched against a database of thousands of candidate lines. The most specific matching rule wins, with less specific rules as fallbacks. Writers can add special cases, running gags and new tracked facts without programmers changing code. The system shipped in Left 4 Dead, Dota and most Valve titles from The Orange Box onward. [abstract] — [Ruskin 2012, "AI-driven Dynamic Dialog through Fuzzy Pattern Matching", GDC](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1015528/AI-driven-Dynamic-Dialog-through); [Short's write-up](https://emshort.blog/2012/03/16/gdc-2012-talk-on-dynamic-dialogue/)
- **Versu: world state is a set of logical sentences.**
  - Simulation state is entirely a set of sentences in **Exclusion Logic**, a modal logic in which `.` and `!` build trees (for example `brown.relationship.lucy.evaluation.attractive!40`). It is stored as a trie, with no objects or pointers in the usual sense.
  - A prefix acts like an object and can be deleted in one step.
  - The `!` operator enforces single-valuedness and automatically removes stale sub-state.
  - The authors say the main advantage is *visibility*: nothing in the simulation state is hidden.

  [read] — [Evans & Short, "The AI Architecture of Versu"](https://versu.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/05/versu.pdf); [Evans, "Introducing Exclusion Logic as a Deontic Logic"](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Introducing-Exclusion-Logic-as-a-Deontic-Logic-Evans/f709fc1a2b2204f9cca24d52a3381ef407f46e02)
- **Versu: social practices.**
  - Practices are parameterized, role-agnostic and can run concurrently. A dinner party and a conversation run at the same time.
  - They offer affordances to agents, who choose by utility over their desires. The authors call the conception of practices "constitutive" (after Rawls): an action exists only inside the practice that makes it available.
  - The drama manager is not a separate kind of entity. It is **just another practice**, an episode-specific, reactive "story manager" that spawns practices and tweaks desires but does not plan ahead. The authors trade emergence at the narrative level for author control.

  [read] — [Evans & Short](https://versu.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/05/versu.pdf)
- **Versu: determinism and undo (directly relevant to time-travel saves).** Versu records the player's chosen actions so an author can reproduce a playthrough exactly, which required keeping the simulator fully deterministic. Its instruction primitives were designed to be cheaply undoable, so agents can evaluate an action's real consequences without copying the whole state. [read] — [Evans & Short](https://versu.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/05/versu.pdf)
- **Versu: production data and costs.**
  - A 20-minute episode took ~2 months, versus Façade's 3+ person-years. A 45–60-minute episode took 6 months.
  - Versu had 1,000+ parameterized actions authored in one year. (Versu compares this with Façade's ~30 discourse acts, which are not the same kind of unit.)
  - The Prompter generator cut a branching 20-minute episode to under a week.
  - Costs: tuning utility across many concurrent practices was described as difficult and unrewarding, and some events need to override everything else that is running.

  [read] — [Evans & Short](https://versu.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/05/versu.pdf)
- **Versu on pure agent simulation.** Agent-driven simulations from Tale-Spin onward repeatedly produced stories without narrative coherence, and authors found it very hard to get narrative control by adjusting individual agents' parameters. [read] — [Evans & Short](https://versu.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/05/versu.pdf)
- **Comme il Faut / Prom Week and Ensemble.** CiF, the social-physics engine behind Prom Week, produced complex narratives unique to each player, steered by players' attempts to reach story goals. [abstract] — [McCoy, Treanor, Samuel, Reed, Mateas & Wardrip-Fruin 2014, "Social Story Worlds With Comme il Faut", IEEE TCIAIG 6(2)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Social-Story-Worlds-With-Comme-il-Faut-Mccoy-Treanor/e1b08605ae0c9e39950f5ce7a97771b71e57b3bc). Ensemble is its successor, built on 5+ years of CiF lessons, with more flexible actions, more expressive rules and an authoring tool. [abstract] — [Samuel, Reed, Maddaloni, Mateas & Wardrip-Fruin 2015, "The Ensemble Engine: Next-Generation Social Physics", FDG](http://www.aaronareed.net/2015/Ensemble-Engine-FDG2015.pdf)
- **Emergent narrative.** Ruth Aylett introduced the term in 1999 for narrative that arises from character interaction, in the manner of improvised drama, rather than being authored. [secondhand] — [Louchart & Aylett, "The Emergent Narrative theoretical investigation"](https://www.macs.hw.ac.uk/~ruth/Papers/narrative/LouchartAylettNile04.pdf). James Ryan's dissertation argues that the raw run of a simulation almost always lacks story structure, and that **curation** (retelling and selecting stories that authentically emerged, rather than imposing structure) is what made works like Dwarf Fortress succeed. It assumes a storyworld made of simulation state plus a trace of events. [read, via summary] — [Ryan 2018, *Curating Simulated Storyworlds*, PhD diss., UC Santa Cruz](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/1340j5h2); [Short's summary/critique](https://emshort.blog/2019/05/21/curating-simulated-storyworlds-james-ryan/)
- **Story sifting.** Story sifters extract compelling narrative from a simulation's chronicle using *sifting patterns*. Winnow is a domain-specific language built on logic programming/Datalog that matches patterns **incrementally** as events stream in, so stories are detected while they unfold rather than only afterward. [abstract] — [Kreminski, Dickinson & Mateas 2021, "Winnow: A Domain-Specific Language for Incremental Story Sifting", AIIDE 17(1):156–163](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/18903); predecessor [Felt (ICIDS 2019)](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-33894-7_27); player-facing use: [Why Are We Like This? (Kreminski et al. 2020)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Why-Are-We-Like-This:-Exploring-Writing-Mechanics-Kreminski-Dickinson/0996a8d2ed852aff7932a336c6ccb4da7b4eb6da)
- **Narrative Substrates.** This framework proposes game architectures that represent, manage and **persist traces of player activity** as unique interactive content in persistent worlds. A one-year deployment of the game *We Ride* found that players highly value first-time and unique events. [abstract] — [Gustafsson, Holme & Mackay 2020, "Narrative Substrates", FDG '20 (Exceptional Paper Award)](https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1145/3402942.3403015)
- **Dwarf Fortress.** Its world generation simulates cultures and creatures interacting over roughly a thousand years and **records the history from the simulation**, which the Legends mode exposes. Tarn Adams wrote "Emergent Narrative in Dwarf Fortress" for *Procedural Storytelling in Game Design* (eds. T. X. Short & T. Adams). [secondhand] — [Characterization and Emergent Narrative in Dwarf Fortress](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/356686095_Characterization_and_Emergent_Narrative_in_Dwarf_Fortress); [Interview with Tarn Adams (Stanford)](https://dhs.stanford.edu/algorithmic-literacy/an-interview-with-tarn-adams/)
- **Ceptre (linear logic).** Ceptre models generative interactive systems as **multiset rewriting**, so gameplay corresponds to proof search in linear logic, with state treated as resources. It builds on earlier linear-logic story specification and lets authors add interactivity selectively. [abstract] — [Martens 2015, *Programming Interactive Worlds with Linear Logic*, CMU-CS-15-134](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~cmartens/thesis/thesis.pdf); [Ceptre (AIIDE 2015)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Ceptre:-A-Language-for-Modeling-Generative-Systems-Martens/c9a163e6083c06a26d1533e423d288af1c5dea1d)

### Inferences
| Model | (A) Core abstraction | (B) Logic/text separation | (C) Long/open-ended scale | (D) Dynamic updates |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Storylets / QBN | State vector + unit DB (pre/effect) + selection architecture | Pre/effects separable; text template inside unit, possibly parameter-bound | **Good**: Fallen London ran for years on this | **Best**: add units without touching others |
| Salience / Valve rules | Fact dictionary + rule DB + specificity ranking | Rules → line IDs; text external | Good for reactive barks; weak arc control | **Very good**: writers add special cases |
| Waypoint | Topic graph + pathfinding + goals | Text on transitions | Conversation scale | Adding nodes improves routing |
| Resource narrative (Kennedy) | *Typed* resources with semantics | Mechanics carry story meaning | Good if resources match the story's grain | Moderate |
| Versu / Praxis | Sentence DB (Exclusion Logic trie) + concurrent practices + utility agents + reactive story manager | Quips = text template + effects; text is coupled per action but generated | Episodes of 20–60 min; replayable across roles | Good; Prompter generation |
| CiF / Ensemble | Social state + social-exchange rules | Templated dialogue | Good replayability | Rules are additive |
| Emergent + sifting (Ryan, Felt/Winnow, DF) | Simulation state + **event chronicle** + pattern queries | Fully separable; telling is curation | **Best** for open-ended/very long (DF ~1,000-year history) | Patterns added any time; incremental matching |
| Ceptre | Multiset-rewriting rules over resource state | Abstract | Good | Rules modular |

- The scaling winners share three structural features: (1) a **queryable world-state store**, (2) **independent units with declarative preconditions and effects**, and (3) a **pluggable selection or curation policy**. These map well onto "composable narrative node packages". A node package should probably be a *bag of preconditioned units plus their scoped flow*, not a sub-graph wired into a global graph.
- An **append-only event chronicle** next to current state is independently motivated by curation and sifting (Ryan, Winnow), persistence (Narrative Substrates), legends and history (Dwarf Fortress), and deterministic replay (Versu). This favors an event-sourced core, where current state is a fold over events, and that matches the time-travel save store.
- Kennedy's critique argues against making every variable an untyped number. The core state model should be **typed** (resources vs. flags vs. relationships vs. beliefs), and Versu's typed, single-valued `!` semantics is a concrete precedent.

### Gaps
- I found no quantitative study comparing authoring cost per hour of play across storylets, branching and simulation, beyond the Façade and Versu self-reports.
- Fallen London's total content size and long-term maintenance data were not found in primary sources.
- Dwarf Fortress's internal history data model is undocumented in academic sources; the claims here are secondhand.

## Q4. Narrative planning and drama management: what state model do they assume?

### Takeaway
Nearly all narrative planners assume a **STRIPS/PDDL-style state**: a closed-world set of ground literals (or fluent/value assignments), actions with preconditions and add/delete effects, outcome goals plus trajectory goals, and solution plans that are sequences or partial orders with causal links. Research extends this with **character intentions** (IPOCL, Glaive), **beliefs and theory of mind** (Sabre, HEADSPACE, IMPRACTICAL), **conflict and failure**, and **partially specified initial states** (ISR). Drama managers add an author **evaluation function** over abstract plot points or beats. Scalability is limited by search cost. Recent work uses LLMs as heuristics over symbolic state, not as a replacement for it.

### Cited Findings
- A narrative planning problem uses the classical tuple ⟨L, I, A, G, f_cost⟩. A state is a set of literals under the closed-world assumption, and an action is ⟨PRE, ADD, DEL⟩. Applying a plan from I yields an execution trace that alternates states and actions. [read] — [Cardona-Rivera et al. 2024](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICAPS/article/download/31509/33669)
- **Points of convergence:**
  - The "fundamental particles" are characters, locations and actions.
  - Goals split into *outcome* goals and *trajectory* goals (author constraints on the path).
  - Actions split into *happenings* (accidents) and *intentional actions* ⟨PRE, ADD, DEL, CHA⟩ carried out by characters.
  - **Causal coherence** supports comprehension; studies show causally coherent plans predict human answers to why/how/what-enabled questions.
  - **Believability** requires *motivational coherence*: intentions of the form intends(c, g).

  [read] — [Cardona-Rivera et al. 2024](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICAPS/article/download/31509/33669); IPOCL primary: [Riedl & Young 2010, "Narrative Planning: Balancing Plot and Character", JAIR 39](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1401.3841)
- **There is no standard specification** for narrative planning problems; different researchers model different narrative phenomena and adapt the representation accordingly. [read] — [Cardona-Rivera et al. 2024](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICAPS/article/download/31509/33669)
- **Glaive** (state-space forward search) lets characters adopt mutually exclusive intentions and fail. Sabre uses a **fluent/value** state, Riedl & Young's intentionality, a model of beliefs and theory of mind, and a model of conflict. [read/abstract] — [Cardona-Rivera et al. 2024](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICAPS/article/download/31509/33669); [Sabre project page](https://cs.uky.edu/~sgware/projects/sabre/); [Ware & Siler 2021, "Sabre", AIIDE](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/download/18896/18661/22662)
- **Initial State Revision (ISR)** partitions the initial state into true, false and undetermined sentences. Undetermined facts are fixed later, whenever convenient for the story, which is possible because the world is synthetic. [read] — [Cardona-Rivera et al. 2024](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICAPS/article/download/31509/33669)
- **Scalability:**
  - Plan-space (POCL) planners give theoretical guarantees but perform poorly.
  - Haslum's semantics-preserving compilation of intentionality into classical planning makes problems **exponentially larger**.
  - Glaive is three orders of magnitude faster than FF on that compilation.
  - Hierarchical (HTN) formalisms are credited with **authorial leverage** (Chen, Nelson & Mateas 2009), because decomposition methods encode scripts and idioms.

  [read] — [Cardona-Rivera et al. 2024](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICAPS/article/download/31509/33669); [Chen, Nelson & Mateas 2009, "Evaluating the Authorial Leverage of Drama Management", AIIDE](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/221251026_Evaluating_the_Authorial_Leverage_of_Drama_Management)
- The open challenges listed include actions that fail, conflict, unrealized threats, deliberate misdirection of audience belief, and the gap between story time and telling time. The survey notes that cognitive models of comprehension describe readers building a **mental concept graph** of events linked by causal, temporal and spatial relations and by intentions. [read] — [Cardona-Rivera et al. 2024](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICAPS/article/download/31509/33669)
- **LLMs and planning.** Farrell & Ware use the Sabre symbolic planner over hand-written domains and let an LLM *guide search* by rating candidate events for plausibility. [abstract] — [Farrell & Ware 2024, "Large Language Models as Narrative Planning Search Guides", IEEE Trans. Games 17(2):419–428](https://scholars.uky.edu/en/publications/large-language-models-as-narrative-planning-search-guides/). Wang & Kreminski find that GPT-4-class models produce causally sound stories only at small scale, and that character intentionality and dramatic conflict remain hard. [abstract] — [Wang & Kreminski 2025, "Can LLMs Generate Good Stories? Insights and Challenges from a Narrative Planning Perspective", IEEE CoG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10161)
- **Drama-management surveys:** Roberts & Isbell 2008, "A survey and qualitative analysis of recent advances in drama management", *Int. Trans. Systems Science and Applications* 4(2):61–75; Nelson, Mateas, Roberts & Isbell 2006, "Declarative Optimization-Based Drama Management in Interactive Fiction", *IEEE CG&A* 26(3):32–41. [citation only, secondhand] — [DODM AAMAS'06 PDF](https://www.kmjn.org/publications/DODM_AAMAS06.pdf). Riedl & Bulitko review about 20 years of interactive narrative and frame it as an intelligent-systems problem. [abstract] — [Riedl & Bulitko 2013, "Interactive Narrative: An Intelligent Systems Approach", *AI Magazine* 34(1):67–77](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1609/aimag.v34i1.2449)
- **Versu's design axes.** Versu places itself on two axes: authorial intent, between hand-authored CYOA and fully emergent narrative, and virtual character autonomy, between strong story and strong autonomy. Façade sits in the middle, and Versu is strongly autonomous with a light, reactive drama manager. [read] — [Evans & Short](https://versu.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/05/versu.pdf)

### Inferences
- Planners and drama managers all assume the same **substrate**: a world state of typed facts, actions or events with preconditions and effects, characters with goals, beliefs and intentions, and an execution trace. The Narrata core should expose exactly this so that planners, drama managers and LLM guides can be **plugged in as selection or "director" policies** without owning the state.
- Requirements for the state model follow directly: support for **per-character beliefs** (state that differs from ground truth), **intentions/goals**, and **undetermined facts** (ISR-style late binding, which helps dynamically updated narratives). A flat global key-value map is not enough.
- Planning is too expensive to run over a whole ultra-long narrative. Evidence points to using it *locally* (a scene or episode) or as heuristic guidance, which is consistent with Versu's reactive, per-episode story managers.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm whether any narrative planner has run online over very long (hours-long or open-ended) interactive play; no evidence was found either way.
- The Roberts & Isbell 2008 and Nelson et al. 2006 IEEE CG&A texts were not read.

## Q5. Formal ontologies and data models for narrative: what entities and relations do they agree on, and which separate narrative structure from media?

### Takeaway
The ontologies agree on a core of **events** (with participants or agents, places and time intervals), **temporal ordering**, **event part-whole relations**, **causal dependency** and **characters/agents**. The drama-oriented ones add **goals, plans, dramatic units, emotions and values**. Several explicitly separate the fabula or story from its media expressions (NOnt via FRBRoo/CIDOC-CRM; the BBC Stories Ontology layered over Event/Timeline ontologies; Object-Based Media's narrative objects that reference media assets). Most of these ontologies are built for **annotation and description**, not runtime simulation. They are a good source for a **storage/export schema**, not a full runtime model.

### Cited Findings
- **NOnt fabula relations:** (i) a *mereological* relation (events contain sub-events; a strict partial order, so acyclic); (ii) a *temporal occurrence* relation mapping each event to a time interval, with intervals related by Allen's 13 interval relations; (iii) *causal dependency*, also a strict partial order (the authors stop short of a full causal theory). NOnt extends CIDOC CRM, FRBRoo and OWL Time, states its axioms in SWRL, draws on LODE and the Simple Event Model (SEM) for event facts, and was used in the Narrative Building and Visualising Tool (NBVT) and the Mingei project. [read] — [Meghini et al. 2021](https://www.semantic-web-journal.net/system/files/swj2539.pdf)
- **BBC Stories Ontology.** It was built as a distillation of OntoMedia and layered on the Event and Timeline ontologies (events with participants, placed on timeline intervals), FOAF for people and GeoNames for places. Its use cases were Doctor Who storylines, Northern Ireland and the Battle of Britain. OntoMedia, inspired by the ABC Ontology and CIDOC CRM, suits annotating *existing* works. The Stories project instead wanted authors to annotate narratives *during production* with minimal effort. A later extension added a **StoryWorld** class (a collection of Stories) and used W3C **PROV** to link story elements to sources and real-world counterparts (wasDerivedFrom). [read] — [Davies, Jewell & Rissen 2013, "Adapting Historical Drama for the Web", NHT'13 @ ACM Hypertext](https://gala.gre.ac.uk/id/eprint/9949/1/9949_DAVIES_%282013%29_%28final_published_version%29_for_GALA.pdf)
- **BBC News Storyline ontology.** It describes a storyline *independently of any article* and uses it to link related developments as a narrative over time. [secondhand] — [Tarling, "Storylines as data in BBC News"](https://medium.com/@jeremytarling/storylines-as-data-in-bbc-news-bd92c25cba6b). BBC R&D's structured-stories work reportedly layers a story **world** (protagonists, places, relationships) under **events**, with telling on top. [secondhand; source page returned 403] — [Ferne, "Designing Structured Stories"](https://medium.com/@tristanf/designing-structured-stories-526277fe7a16)
- **Drammar.** It models agents (who have goals and intend plans), actions (intentional processes contained in units), plans (action sequences toward goals), **units** (dramatic segments that form a hierarchy), emotions and values. It consists of an OWL conceptual model plus SWRL rules. [abstract] — [Lombardo, Damiano & Pizzo, Drammar (ISWC 2018)](https://www.cirma.unito.it/drammar/drammarlode/)
- **Object-Based Media authoring (Ursu et al. 2020).**
  - The architecture has a **production-agnostic narrative engine** that runs each story's logic for each viewing, a **composition engine** that assembles media into a stream on the narrative engine's instructions, and a media repository.
  - *Atomic objects* reference media assets (video, audio, text, graphics, data) and carry metadata about content and narrative function.
  - *Narrative objects* are built **recursively** from conditional aggregation structures, such as conditional forks with default rules. A whole production is itself a narrative object.
  - Conditions refer to object properties, metadata, interaction variables and internal variables.
  - The authors note that earlier interactive films hard-coded their logic in low-level code, so programmers had to translate between creatives and code.

  [read] — [Ursu, Smith, Hook et al. 2020, "Authoring Interactive Fictional Stories in Object-Based Media (OBM)", ACM IMX 2020, pp. 127–137](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/163696/)
- **Persistent worlds from narrative text (2026).** A 2026 preprint extracts explicit, persistent world representations (entities, locations, relationships, evolving world states) from narrative text and reuses one world model across several playable instantiations. [abstract] — [Chen 2026, "Reconstructing Persistent Worlds from Narratives for Narrative-Grounded Interactive Experiences", arXiv:2608.04037](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04037)

### Inferences
- Shared minimal schema across ontologies: **Event**(id, time interval, place, participants with roles, sub-events, causes) + **Agent/Character** + **Place** + **Fabula/StoryWorld** container + **Narration/Expression** referencing fabula fragments. This is a good candidate for Narrata's *portable interchange schema* across PostgreSQL, a graph DB or JSON, because it is already proven in RDF (graph), and its relations (strict partial orders, interval relations) fit relational tables just as well.
- The ontologies record *what happened and how it is told*. They lack runtime constructs: preconditions and effects, selection policy, and per-agent belief state. Narrata needs both: an ontology-like **record layer** (events and relations, good for export and for long-narrative querying) and an operational **rule layer** (Q3/Q4).
- OBM is the clearest real-world precedent for "narrative engine ≠ media": a reusable, production-agnostic engine operates over recursive narrative objects that only *reference* media. Recursive composition (a production is itself a narrative object) matches Narrata's composable node packages.

### Gaps
- OntoMedia, SEM, the Fabula ontology, GOLEM (2025) and the "Ontology of interactive narrative" were not read in primary form; I cannot list their exact classes.
- I could not reach BBC pages (bbc.co.uk was blocked for fetching), so the Storyline ontology class list is unverified.
- I found no ontology that models *interactive* runtime state (choices, variables, branches) as rigorously as NOnt models fabula. OBM's conditional structures are the closest.

## Q6. LLM-era research on long-form story state and continuity (2023–2026): structured state vs. text, and graph stores for long narratives

### Takeaway
The 2023–2026 literature agrees that **free-text memory degrades with length**. Facts drift silently when summaries are rewritten, and fluent models still break commitments over long interactive horizons. The best-performing designs keep a **typed, keyed, compact structured state** (character snapshots, event records, open "promises"), or they run a **symbolic engine for state and actions** with the LLM as generator or realizer. Knowledge graphs help but carry a maintenance cost. This is strong recent evidence for a logic-first core with text as a *projection*, and it supports pluggable storage, since the structured state is small relative to the prose.

### Cited Findings
- **NstAgent (2026 preprint).** It tracks a typed state with three parts: character snapshots (location, goals, relationships, knowledge, possessions, condition), past events not explicit in the outline, and *future requirements* (planted clues, promised confrontations). Four JSON operations maintain it: upsert character, add event, add requirement, resolve requirement. Consistency stays stable from 10K to 100K words while a free-text rolling-summary baseline degrades. Cost grows roughly linearly. The final state at 100K words holds about a dozen characters. The authors attribute the gains to entries being typed and keyed rather than free text. [abstract/html] — [Wan & Chen 2026, "Scaling Long-Form Story Generation via Narrative State Tracking", arXiv:2609.35759](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.35759)
- **Baselines in the same paper.** KG-based DOME needs very many per-triple LLM memory calls (described as thousands per 10K-word story). Compressed-history methods (StoryWriter, RollSum) lose or silently change details. Outline methods (Re3, DOC) are static and cannot react to details that emerge during writing. [abstract/html, secondhand for the baselines] — [Wan & Chen 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.35759)
- **DOME.** It pairs a dynamic hierarchical outline grounded in narrative theory with a memory module built on **temporal knowledge graphs**, and uses a Temporal Conflict Analyzer to evaluate consistency. [read via survey] — [Teleki et al. 2025, "A Survey on LLMs for Story Generation", Findings of EMNLP 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.750.pdf); primary: [Wang et al. 2025, NAACL](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.13575)
- **Survey findings (Teleki et al. 2025).** LLMs keep local coherence but struggle with global coherence (plotlines, character development, themes) across long texts. Most systems use templated prompting with semi-structured inputs (character tuples, story states, user attributes). This favors control and interpretability but depends on manual, human-in-the-loop steps. StoryVerse turns author-defined "abstract acts" with prerequisites into character actions in a game. [read] — [Teleki et al. 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.750.pdf)
- **NCP-Bench (2026).** It contains 100 interactive narrative environments derived from movie synopses, each with machine-checkable facts, commitments and trajectories. The best model survived only 42% of runs after 20 turns, fact-conflict rates ranged from 40% to 68%, and the authors conclude that linguistic quality does not guarantee commitment preservation. [abstract] — [Ma et al. 2026, "Can LLM Agents Stick to the Script? A Benchmark for Long-Horizon Consistency in Interactive Narratives", ICML 2026, arXiv:2608.08160](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08160)
- **STORY2GAME.** LLM-generated **preconditions and effects** decide which game state must be tracked. A game engine executes the actions symbolically, and the system generates new actions at runtime (revising earlier ones) when players attempt things outside the story. [abstract] — [Zhou, Basavatia, Siam, Chen & Riedl 2025, arXiv:2505.03547](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03547)
- **WhatELSE.** Authors shape a narrative *possibility space* at configurable levels of abstraction (pivot, outline and variant views), and LLM-based narrative planning unfolds it into events during play. In a user study (N=12), authors could perceive and edit the space. [abstract] — [Lu, Zhou & Wang 2025, "WhatELSE", CHI 2025, arXiv:2502.18641](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18641)
- **Elsewise.** When LLMs generate continuations, the gap between author intent and player experience widens. Elsewise visualizes possibility spaces as "bundled storylines", which improved authors' ability to anticipate what players experience (N=12). [abstract] — [Wang, Chung, Roemmele, … Kreminski 2025/2026, "Elsewise", arXiv:2601.15295](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15295)
- **Generative Agents.** Each agent keeps a *memory stream*, a complete natural-language record of its experiences, which is retrieved by recency, importance and relevance and synthesized into reflections and plans. The world is a natural-language tree of areas and objects, with 25 agents. [abstract] — [Park et al. 2023, "Generative Agents: Interactive Simulacra of Human Behavior", arXiv:2304.03442 (UIST 2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03442)
- **Agents' Room.** It decomposes long-fiction writing into specialized planning and writing agents (plot, character, language) and was preferred by expert raters over baselines. It also introduces the "Tell Me A Story" dataset. [abstract] — [Huot et al. 2025, "Agents' Room", ICLR 2025, arXiv:2410.02603](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02603)
- **Knowledge graph plus narratology.** A 2025 preprint builds a knowledge graph with long-term and short-term memory, plus a narratology-based theme/obstacle framework, to reduce theme drift in long stories. [abstract] — [Shi, Huang & Feng 2025, arXiv:2508.03137](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03137). PlotGraph builds and rule-validates a symbolic plot graph of characters, events and locations *before* generating a screenplay. [secondhand] — [PlotGraph (Springer, 2026)](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-032-31673-8_30)

### Inferences
- Narrata's thesis is well supported here: a deterministic logic core that owns typed state, events and commitments, with LLM or text realizers as *clients*. That is the architecture the 2025–2026 literature is converging on (NstAgent, STORY2GAME, StoryVerse, Farrell & Ware).
- **"Future requirements / commitments"** (planted setups awaiting payoff) appear as a first-class state type in NstAgent and NCP-Bench. Narrata should treat **open narrative obligations** (Chekhov's guns, promises, foreshadowing) as core state, not as authoring notes, because they are what long narratives lose first.
- Graph stores: KGs work for long-story memory but are expensive to maintain at fine granularity (DOME). Typed, keyed records are cheaper and work as well or better. The data supports **storage-agnostic typed records**, with graph indexing as an *optional query accelerator* (for relationship and causality traversal over long histories), not as a required backend.
- Dynamic updates in the LLM era (STORY2GAME's runtime action generation) need a core that can **accept new action schemas at runtime** and re-validate earlier ones. That argues for content units being data (declarative pre/effects), not compiled code.

### Gaps
- Dramatron (Mirowski et al., CHI 2023: hierarchical logline → characters/beats → dialogue), Re3 (Yang et al., EMNLP 2022) and DOC (Yang et al., ACL 2023) were not read; they are referenced only as baselines here. Dramatron's structure is described from memory and is unverified this session.
- Several key 2026 items (NstAgent, Chen 2026) are recent arXiv preprints without confirmed peer review; NCP-Bench is reported as accepted to ICML 2026.
- I found no study that compares graph-DB vs. relational vs. document storage for narrative state at scale. The storage question is unaddressed in the academic literature.

## Q7. Surveys of interactive narrative and story-generation representations (classic and 2020–2026)

### Takeaway
The classic surveys frame interactive narrative around three tensions: **authorial intent vs. character autonomy vs. player modeling** (Riedl & Bulitko), **plot vs. space automation** with a call for a standard narrative model (Kybartas & Bidarra), and **search or optimization over plot abstractions** (Roberts & Isbell; DODM). The 2024 planning survey confirms there is **no standard problem specification**. LLM-era surveys find that **global coherence** is the central unsolved problem and that structured inputs dominate in practice.

### Cited Findings
- Riedl & Bulitko 2013 lay out roughly 20 years of AI approaches to interactive narrative. [abstract] — [AI Magazine 34(1)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1609/aimag.v34i1.2449)
- Kybartas & Bidarra 2017 sort systems into manual authoring, plot generation, space generation, and full story generation, and argue for a *standard model of narrative*. [abstract] — [IEEE TCIAIG 9(3)](https://repository.tudelft.nl/record/uuid:0e967ade-b728-4c3f-b3bd-5b89a35b8e2b)
- Cardona-Rivera et al. 2024 report no standard for narrative planning problems, convergence on characters, locations and actions, causal and motivational coherence as core qualities, and three planning paradigms (plan-space, hierarchical, heuristic search). [read] — [ICAPS 2024](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICAPS/article/download/31509/33669)
- Kreminski & Wardrip-Fruin 2018 provide the storylet design-space survey. [read] — [ICIDS 2018](https://mkremins.github.io/publications/Storylets_SketchingAMap.pdf)
- Teleki et al. 2025 survey LLM story generation (independent vs. author-assistance), name global coherence as the main open problem, and note a lack of shared benchmarks for interactive and adaptive storytelling. [read] — [Findings of EMNLP 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.750.pdf)
- Roberts & Isbell 2008 is a drama-management survey. [citation only] — see [DODM AAMAS'06](https://www.kmjn.org/publications/DODM_AAMAS06.pdf) for related context.

### Inferences
- No survey identifies a single agreed-upon "core" representation. The recurring abstractions are world state + events/actions (pre/effects) + agents (goals/beliefs) + an author-quality signal + a content-selection architecture. The lack of a standard is itself an opening for Narrata's portable content-pack format.

### Gaps
- I found no 2020–2026 survey devoted specifically to *runtime data representations* for interactive narrative (as distinct from generation techniques). The INDCOR white papers (e.g., [arXiv:2010.10135](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.10135)) were seen but not read.
- Alhussain & Azmi 2021 ("Automatic Story Generation: A Survey of Approaches", ACM CSUR, [link](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3453156)) was not read.

## Q8. Critiques: are text and logic inseparable in practice? The authoring bottleneck and the risks of over-abstraction

### Takeaway
The counter-evidence does not show that logic and text *cannot* be separated; Façade, SceneMaker, OBM and Versu all separate them to useful degrees. It shows that separation **moves cost and risk** elsewhere:
- Authoring becomes state design and tuning work (Versu, Kennedy, Short).
- Much of the experienced quality lives in text **variants keyed to state** (Façade's tone alternates, Versu's adverbial modifiers), so the text layer needs rich read access to state.
- Writers prefer **text-first** tools (ink).
- The medium **constrains** what plot can be told (Elliott, via Cardona-Rivera).
- Pure simulation or abstraction yields **incoherent stories** without curation or direction (Tale-Spin, Ryan).
- Real productions hit an authoring and production wall (Façade, OBM).

### Cited Findings
- **Authoring as the bottleneck.** Authoring is still considered a bottleneck for interactive storytelling, and the problems go beyond interface design. [abstract] — [Spierling & Szilas 2009, "Authoring Issues beyond Tools", ICIDS](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-10643-9_9); see also [*The Authoring Problem* (Springer, 2022), introduction](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-05214-9_1)
- **Façade's authoring burden.** Every jdb (dialogue, staging, emotion, gesture) was hand-authored rather than generated from higher-level behaviors, so Façade generates sequences, not sentences. The authors propose a higher-level authoring tool that *compiles abstract narrative specifications* into ABL behaviors, and say that otherwise rare hybrid programmer-writers are required. [read] — [Mateas & Stern 2005](https://users.soe.ucsc.edu/~michaelm/publications/mateas-aiide2005.pdf)
- **Text variants carry experienced coherence (Façade).** Most jdbs have 3–5 alternates for different affinity and tension combinations and 2–4 equivalent phrasings per tone. Because tone always matches, players *perceive causal links between causally independent units*. [read] — [Mateas & Stern 2005](https://users.soe.ucsc.edu/~michaelm/publications/mateas-aiide2005.pdf)
- **Text as an expressive medium (Versu).** Versu chose text output not only because it is cheaper than 3D animation but because it is *more expressive*. Adverbial modifiers ("answered the door reluctantly") reveal internal conflict and personality at almost no cost, where a 3D game would need new animations for each modifier. Each Versu dialogue unit (quip) is a text template bundled with social and emotional effects. [read] — [Evans & Short](https://versu.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/05/versu.pdf)
- **Writers' tools are text-first.** inkle's ink is markup-first: prose comes first and logic is inserted inline, and Jon Ingold designed it so writers could work fluently. [secondhand] — [inkle: ink](https://www.inklestudios.com/ink/); [Ingold, "Introduction to Ink"](https://medium.com/game-writing-guide/introduction-to-ink-3e6c224865f8)
- **Form and content.** Some researchers argue that discourse and narration cannot be separated from plot content (Elliott 2004) and build systems that co-evolve them (BIPOCL, PLOTSHOT). [read] — [Cardona-Rivera et al. 2024](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICAPS/article/download/31509/33669)
- **Production reality (OBM).** In a well-funded interactive film, the production company found the interactive workflow extremely challenging, and writing was the hardest part. Planned structures had to be dropped or simplified, the endings were reduced for cost, and turning abstract structures into footage exposed a need for "shooting grammars". [read] — [Ursu et al. 2020](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/163696/)
- **Over-uniform abstraction (Kennedy 2017).** Treating every quality the same erased useful distinctions and forced later workarounds. [read via summary] — [Kennedy 2017](https://weatherfactory.biz/qbn-to-resource-narratives/)
- **Storylet and salience weaknesses (Short).** QBN is dull until there is critical mass and needs manual bookkeeping. Salience can accidentally unlock unintended outcomes. [read via summary] — [Short 2016](https://emshort.blog/2016/04/12/beyond-branching-quality-based-and-salience-based-narrative-structures/)
- **Simulation without structure.** Agent-driven simulations repeatedly lacked narrative coherence (Versu on Tale-Spin), and raw simulation traces almost always lack story structure, so curation is needed (Ryan). [read] — [Evans & Short](https://versu.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/05/versu.pdf); [Ryan 2018 via Short](https://emshort.blog/2019/05/21/curating-simulated-storyworlds-james-ryan/)
- **Tuning cost.** Tuning utility scores across many concurrent practices was hard and unrewarding even with an inspector tool. [read] — [Evans & Short](https://versu.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/05/versu.pdf)
- **Author intent drift with LLMs.** With LLM continuation, the gap between author intent and player experience widens, which motivated possibility-space visualization tools. [abstract] — [Elsewise, arXiv:2601.15295](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15295)

### Inferences
- The practical answer to "text and logic are inseparable" is **co-location at authoring time, separation at runtime**. Authors write a unit's prose, variants and logic together in one text-first file (the ink and quip style). The compiler splits that into (a) declarative pre/effects and flow for the core and (b) a keyed text or template table for realizers. This keeps SceneMaker- and OBM-style runtime separation without losing writer fluency. Façade's own conclusion, a tool that compiles abstract specifications into behaviors, points the same way.
- The core must give realizers a **rich, read-only state view**: affinity, tension, beliefs, the current discourse/reveal state and parameter bindings. Variant selection is where experienced coherence comes from.
- Over-abstraction risk: a fully abstract, untyped core will push authors into bookkeeping. Typed domain primitives (resources, relationships, beliefs, commitments, tension/arc values) and authoring tools (inspector, deterministic replay, possibility-space views) count as part of the "core" in practice (Versu's Inspector and Playback; Elsewise/WhatELSE).

### Gaps
- Procedural rhetoric (Bogost) and Mateas's procedural-literacy arguments were not researched this session.
- I found no controlled study of writer productivity comparing text-first and logic-first authoring formats.

## Q9. Synthesis: what is the right abstract core for a narrative engine, given this evidence?

### Takeaway
No single model in the literature is "the core". The evidence converges on a **layered core**:
1. A **typed world-state store** (facts, resources, relationships, per-agent beliefs and intentions, open commitments).
2. An **append-only event chronicle** with causal, temporal and part-whole relations (this is the fabula).
3. **Declarative content units** with preconditions, effects, parameters and scoped flow (storylets, beats, practices, scenes), where statecharts and flow graphs live *inside* units.
4. **Pluggable content-selection or director policies** (player choice, salience, weighted, drama manager with evaluation function, planner, sifter, LLM-guided).
5. A **discourse layer** that tracks what has been told and revealed, to whom, and in what order.
6. **Realizer adapters** (text templates, LLM, media) that consume a state view and emit presentation.

Graphs are an authoring view and a local control structure, not the substrate. Storage can stay pluggable because both the ontologies (RDF graphs) and the runtime systems (trie of sentences, JSON records, Datalog) encode the same logical content.

### Cited Findings
- State + units + selection is the storylet and drama-management common denominator. [read] — [Kreminski & Wardrip-Fruin 2018](https://mkremins.github.io/publications/Storylets_SketchingAMap.pdf); [Mateas & Stern 2005](https://users.soe.ucsc.edu/~michaelm/publications/mateas-aiide2005.pdf)
- World as a visible set of typed sentences, with deterministic replay and undoable primitives. [read] — [Evans & Short (Versu)](https://versu.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/05/versu.pdf)
- Event chronicle with incremental pattern queries for long-running worlds. [abstract] — [Winnow 2021](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/18903); persistence of traces: [Narrative Substrates 2020](https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1145/3402942.3403015)
- Fabula with mereological, temporal (Allen) and causal relations, separated from its narrations. [read] — [Meghini et al. 2021](https://www.semantic-web-journal.net/system/files/swj2539.pdf)
- Plot / discourse / narration layers; discourse in audience-belief space; planners rely on state, actions, intentions and beliefs. [read] — [Cardona-Rivera et al. 2024](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICAPS/article/download/31509/33669)
- Typed, keyed narrative state (including future requirements) beats free-text memory at 100K words. [abstract] — [Wan & Chen 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.35759); commitment-preservation failures: [NCP-Bench 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08160)
- Production-agnostic narrative engine plus composition engine plus recursive narrative objects that reference media. [read] — [Ursu et al. 2020](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/163696/)
- Statechart-based separation of dialogue content from logic. [abstract] — [Gebhard et al. 2012](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12193-011-0077-1)

### Inferences
- **Mapping to Narrata's existing pieces** (deterministic runtime, flow VM, statecharts, time-travel save store, composable node packages):
  - *Deterministic runtime + time-travel store* → event-sourced state: chronicle = source of truth, state = fold. Versu's precedent: determinism for exact replay and undoable primitives for cheap lookahead and branching. Branching saves are alternative chronicles (Koenitz's "products" of one protostory).
  - *Flow VM + statecharts* → the **internal control of a content unit** (a beat, scene or practice), with history and interruption/resume semantics (SceneMaker, Façade gist points). Not the global story structure.
  - *Composable node packages* → bags of **declarative, preconditioned units** plus typed state schemas plus optional selection policies. They are recursively composable like OBM narrative objects, and role-parameterized like Versu practices so they can be reused across characters.
  - *Pluggable storage* → define the logical model (typed facts, events, relations, units) independently. A graph DB is an optional index for relationship and causal traversal and sifting queries; relational or JSON stores hold the same records. Neither the literature nor the precedent systems require a particular backend.
  - *Separation from text/media* → yes at runtime, but (i) add a **discourse/reveal layer** to the core, (ii) give realizers a **rich read-only state view** for variant selection, (iii) allow **co-located, text-first authoring** that compiles into logic plus keyed text, and (iv) let realizers declare **medium constraints** back to discourse.
- **Ultra-long and dynamically updated narratives:** the evidence favors (1) independent units over global graphs, so new content does not require editing old content; (2) typed state plus open-commitment tracking; (3) incremental (streaming) queries over the chronicle rather than whole-history scans (Winnow); (4) runtime-addable unit and action schemas with re-validation (STORY2GAME); and (5) late-bound facts (ISR-style undetermined initial state) so later content can define backstory without contradicting what has already been told.
- **Main risk to manage:** over-abstraction that turns authors into bookkeepers (Kennedy, Short, Versu tuning). Mitigations from the literature include typed domain primitives, author-facing inspectors and deterministic replay (Versu), possibility-space visualization (Elsewise, WhatELSE), and fallback-capable salience (Valve).

### Gaps
- No academic source evaluates an event-sourced, storage-pluggable narrative runtime end-to-end; this recommendation is an inference that combines separate findings.
- Evidence on narratives longer than ~100K words or running for years (live-service narrative such as Fallen London) is anecdotal or from practitioners, not academic.
- I found no empirical comparison of how discourse-layer modeling (reveal and audience-belief tracking) affects authoring cost in shipped games.
