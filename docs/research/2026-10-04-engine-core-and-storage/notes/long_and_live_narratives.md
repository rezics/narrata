# Ultra-long (超长叙事) and dynamically-updated (动态更新的叙事) narratives: real-world cases, failure modes, and what they demand of a narrative engine core and storage

Research date: 2026-10-04. Scope: live-service and episodic narrative, very large single-player RPGs, canon/continuity databases, LLM-era dynamic narrative and memory, hot reload / live authoring, and engineering and verification patterns at scale. Each section ends with inferences aimed at Narrata (Rust; deterministic runtime, flow VM, statecharts, time-travel save store, program versioning and save migrations, composable node packages, Wasm reader).

Source-quality legend: (P) = primary (developer/publisher docs, official blog, paper); (S) = secondary press or wiki; (snippet) = search-result summary only. The page itself was not fetched or did not render, so treat as lower confidence.

## Q1. Live-service and episodic narrative: how do long-running games add content, keep player state compatible, and carry choices across episodes and seasons?

### Takeaway
Long-running narrative services rarely migrate the *execution position*. What they persist and evolve is a *world-state of facts* (qualities, flags, quest completion), and they treat content as additive, guarded units. When content has to change or be removed, operators use compensating migrations: abandon and re-accept a quest, auto-advance to the next quest, refund and respec, phase players into per-progress world views, or let players restate their choices through a "story generator". Removing content outright, as Destiny's vault did, is the costliest failure mode for players. Bungie reversed it in 2026, and it is still technically hard to undo.

### Cited Findings

**Fallen London / StoryNexus / QBN (2009–present, continuously extended)**
- Fallen London (launched 2009 as *Echo Bazaar*) contains **over 4.5 million words** ("about five complete Shakespeares"). It had **70,000 words by December 2009**, and Failbetter was incorporated on 11 Jan 2010. — [Failbetter: Fallen London's 15th Birthday (P, Jan 2025)](https://www.failbettergames.com/news/fallen-londons-15th-birthday)
- Growth curve: ~10,000 words at launch, >1,000,000 by 2014, ~3,000,000 by the 10th anniversary (2019). "Thousands of new sentences in many hundreds of storylets are added each year." Less than a year after launch, the developers had identified "sixty distinct narrative patterns" buildable in StoryNexus. The fan wiki reached ~25,000 pages. Long arcs such as "Seeking Mr Eaten's Name" span more than a year of real-time play. — [IF50: 2009: Fallen London (Aaron Reed)](https://if50.substack.com/p/2009-fallen-london). Earlier figures: ~1.5M words (~2015) and ~2.5M (2020), per search summary of [Unwinnable 2020](https://unwinnable.com/2020/06/05/still-fallen-london-127/) (snippet).
- QBN definition: storylets are "unlocked by qualities". Qualities are numeric variables representing "everything from inventory to skills to story progress". — [SimpleQBN: Quality-Based Narrative](https://videlais.github.io/simple-qbn/qbn.html). Alexis Kennedy later proposed the name "resource narrative" because "quality" was misunderstood (same source).
- Storylets are "a database of discrete, reorderable narrative 'chunks' or 'modules'". In StoryNexus games such as Fallen London, "nearly all storylets are location-bound". Starfreighter's *parametrized storylets* "treat the game state as a database", binding characters and items to named parameters. — [Kreminski & Wardrip-Fruin, "Sketching a Map of the Storylets Design Space", ICIDS 2018 (P)](https://mkremins.github.io/publications/Storylets_SketchingAMap.pdf)
- Expandability of QBN: "It's possible to drop in modular elements of new content alongside old content and be relatively sanguine about how you're affecting the rest of the system." Costs: heavy manual quality bookkeeping, content surfacing gets harder as the storylet DB grows, and the form is "hard on new authors because the content tends to be uninteresting until there are a fair number of storylets". Salience-based systems are similarly "relatively easy to build a rudimentary set of content with sensible, broad defaults, and then gradually add new, more salient content", but are "hard to test" (King of Chicago). — [Emily Short, "Beyond Branching" (P, 2016)](https://emshort.blog/2016/04/12/beyond-branching-quality-based-and-salience-based-narrative-structures/)
- Bruno Dias (IF writer/developer; his Failbetter affiliation was not verified in this session) on QBN engine design: "90% of everything about storylets is static, but the 10% of variable content and scripting is very important to making the whole thing work". He splits a QBN system into narrative engine / text engine / UI engine and warns that "DSLs also threaten to balloon into general-purpose programming languages." — [Bruno Dias, "Attempted: Building a general-purpose QBN system" (P, 2017)](https://brunodias.dev/2017/05/30/an-ideal-qbn-system.html)

**Destiny 2: content removal ("Destiny Content Vault")**
- With *Beyond Light* (10 Nov 2020), the Red War, Curse of Osiris and Warmind campaigns, several seasonal narratives, and destinations (Io, Titan, Mars, Mercury, Leviathan) were removed. The reasons given were technical: install size and maintainability ("an ever-growing live service game will eventually reach a critical mass in file size"). — [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/destiny-2/campaign-removed); [DestinyTracker](https://destinytracker.com/destiny-2/articles/bungie-plans-vault-destiny-2-content-year-4-beyond) (S). The game exceeded 100 GB at a time when many consoles had 500 GB drives (DestinyTracker, S).
- The vault caused lasting player harm and even legal trouble. — [Windows Central (title/summary only)](https://www.windowscentral.com/gaming/bungie-vaulting-destiny-2-content-backfires-with-the-latest-red-war-lawsuit-court-ruling) (snippet). On **21 Sep 2026** Bungie ("The Next Chapter for Bungie") committed to unvaulting the D2 campaigns, destinations and raids. Josh Deane: "I wish we could just flip a freaking switch…". No date was given, and the work was described as technically difficult after the 2026 layoffs. — [GamerHub](https://gamerhub.co.uk/bungie-destiny-2-unvaulting-red-war/); [Rogue](https://www.rogue.site/news/bungie-announces-a-return-for-destinys-vaulted-content-and-a-future-f/) (S; Bungie's own post not fetched).

**Final Fantasy XIV: a world rebooted, then a main story pruned in place**
- The original 1.0 servers shut down on **11 Nov 2012**. The in-fiction justification was the "Seventh Umbral Calamity". 1.0 character data was transferred to *A Realm Reborn*. "Legacy" characters got altered MSQ dialogue and an exclusive intro, plus marks and perks. — [Final Fantasy Wiki: FFXIV (version 1.0)](https://finalfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Final_Fantasy_XIV_(version_1.0)); [Wikipedia: FFXIV (2010)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Final_Fantasy_XIV_(2010_video_game)) (S)
- Patch 5.3 (2020) revised or removed ARR main-scenario quests. Official migration rule for in-flight players: "you may find a notification asking you to abandon the quest. If the quest has been changed, you will also be asked to reaccept it. If the quest was subject to removal, you will be asked to accept the next quest of the main scenario." Two quests changed in earlier patches (4.0, 4.4) required manual abandon/re-accept. Square Enix also stated "these adjustments will not affect the overall story." — [FFXIV Patch 5.3 Notes (P)](https://na.finalfantasyxiv.com/lodestone/topics/detail/c2f8c5ba14d9cfaa09d5aab63c5c2da260eae21a). Roughly 13% of 2.0–2.5 quests were removed. — [ResetEra summary](https://www.resetera.com/threads/details-on-ffxiv-5-3-arr-revamp-13-of-quests-removed-from-2-0-2-5-increased-exp-from-msq-flying-in-base-zones-crystal-tower-raid-now-required.191034/); [ConsoleGamesWiki: Main Scenario Revisions](https://ffxiv.consolegameswiki.com/wiki/Main_Scenario_Revisions) (S)
- From 6.1, older MSQ duties were converted from 8-player to 4-player or solo so that the Duty Support NPC system could run them. — [ConsoleGamesWiki](https://ffxiv.consolegameswiki.com/wiki/Main_Scenario_Revisions) (S)

**World of Warcraft: per-player world views and selectable timelines**
- Phasing (fully used from *Wrath of the Lich King*) "causes the appearance of some outdoor areas to drastically change from the player's perspective, based on quest progress". Example: after the Wrathgate questline, a player sees Alexstrasza and the area on fire, while players who haven't done it see the old state in the same location. — [Warcraft Wiki: Phasing](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Phasing) (S)
- Chromie Time lets a player pick which expansion "timeline" to level through. The player is phased into a different instance where creatures scale to their level, and players on different timelines can't see each other in the same zone. — [Warcraft Wiki: Chromie Time (quest)](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Chromie_Time_(quest)); [Blizzard forums](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/chromie-time/841768) (S)

**Gacha live-service (Genshin Impact)**

**Episodic choice carry-over (Telltale, Mass Effect, Dragon Age, Life is Strange)**
- Telltale's *The Walking Dead* S3 (Dec 2016) had three paths. (1) Import a local S2 save "and all your choices will be imported along with it". (2) Migrate via a Telltale account and cloud saves. (3) A "story generator": "go through a series of questions and set whatever world state you want, starting Season 3 at one of **42 possible start points**." — [PCGamesN (S, 13 Dec 2016)](https://www.pcgamesn.com/the-walking-dead-season-three/the-walking-dead-season-3-save-game-transfers). - Mass Effect 3: Casey Hudson said "Numerically, it's over **1,000 variables** that we'll have access to for shaping the Mass Effect 3 experience." The import chains ME1 → ME2 → ME3, so ME1 choices with no ME2 effect can surface in ME3. — [Game Rant](https://gamerant.com/mass-effect-3-1000-variables/); [Destructoid](https://www.destructoid.com/mass-effect-3-gets-complicated-with-1000-variables/) (S). *Mass Effect: Genesis* (an interactive comic) lets players without saves set the major decisions. — [Mass Effect Wiki: Save File Transfer](https://masseffect.fandom.com/wiki/Save_File_Transfer) (S)
- Dragon Age Keep: a cloud web app where players edit a "Tapestry" of **300+ decisions** from DA:O and DA2, which DA:Inquisition imports via EA account login. — [Dragon Age Wiki: Dragon Age Keep](https://dragonage.fandom.com/wiki/Dragon_Age_Keep) (S). *The Veilguard* (2024) dropped the Keep. The player restates the original ending ("We grew apart" vs "She died") in an early dialogue, and the game "respects both endings". — [Game Rant](https://gamerant.com/life-is-strange-double-exposure-respects-both-original-endings/) (S)
- Divinity: Original Sin 2 *Definitive Edition* was shipped as a separate client precisely so saves would not need to carry over: "Because of all the changes made in the Definitive Edition, save games will not carry over." — [Larian Support FAQ (P)](https://larian.com/support/faqs/will-my-saves-carry-over-from-the-classic-version-to-the-definitive-edition-9)

**Mobile interactive-story catalogs**
- *Choices* (Pixelberry, Aug 2016): ~90+ "books", each typically 15–19 chapters, released as weekly episodes. All stories are written in-house. — [Wikipedia: Choices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choices:_Stories_You_Play) (S)
- *Episode* (Pocket Gems, 2014): a UGC platform with a proprietary scripting language. ### Inferences
- **The cross-version contract is facts, not positions.** Every successful carry-over above (Telltale, ME's 1,000+ variables, Dragon Age's 300+ decisions, LiS's single restated choice, FFXIV's "accept the next MSQ quest") moves a *declared set of world-state facts*. None restores a mid-scene program counter. Narrata's save model should make the **fact store the durable, migratable artifact** and treat flow-VM/statechart positions as *derivable or resettable to safe points*.
- **Export/import manifests per package.** Each narrative package (episode, season, expansion) should declare an *export schema*: the facts later packages may read, plus defaults and a "generator" (questionnaire) path for players without saves. The Telltale "42 start points" and ME *Genesis* are exactly this. The Veilguard cut 300+ inputs down to about three, which shows that the import surface is a design budget and should be explicit and versioned.
- **Compensating migrations are normal operations.** FFXIV's abandon/re-accept/skip-to-next rules and Cyberpunk's refund-and-respec are migrations that preserve player investment while invalidating positions. Narrata's migration DSL should support (a) remapping a position to the nearest valid safe point, (b) granting or refunding facts, (c) emitting a player-visible notice, and (d) per-save version stamps, since FFXIV still had to handle quests "changed before Patch 4.0".
- **Never hard-delete content that facts refer to.** Destiny shows that unloading content to save space is reasonable, but losing it is not. Content packages should be **content-addressed and archivable/cold-storable** (unload ≠ delete), and the fact store must never hold dangling references. Use tombstones that point to a replacement or "archived" package.
- **Per-player world views are a projection of facts.** WoW phasing (the world rendered from quest-progress facts) and Chromie Time (user-selected timeline) suggest a shared-world store layered as *global timeline facts + per-player overlay facts + timeline selector*. Narrata's statecharts and fact queries can express phase predicates directly.
- **QBN/storylets is the most proven architecture for indefinitely additive content** (15+ years, 4.5M words). Its weak points are discoverability, bookkeeping and testing, so they must be met with tooling: static analysis over quality predicates and reachability queries (see Q6).

### Gaps
- No primary GDC/Failbetter source found on Fallen London's *backend* (server stack, how qualities are stored, how deploys interact with live sessions). Only a forum post on the redesign surfaced ([Failbetter forum](https://community.failbettergames.com/t/dev-blog-insights-into-the-fallen-london-redesign/18507)).
- Fortnite seasonal story/live events, Honkai: Star Rail, Sunless Sea/Skies: not researched within budget.
- **Narrative A/B testing** and **streaming/downloading story chunks** (e.g., Choices/Episode chapter delivery): no primary source found.
- Branch analytics (e.g., Telltale's end-of-episode choice percentages) are well known but were not sourced here.
- Bungie's own unvault blog post was not fetched; the 2026 details come from secondary press.

## Q2. Very large single-player narratives: scale (words, nodes, flags), how state is tracked, and what tooling broke

### Takeaway
AAA narrative RPGs converge on a **global fact database**: numeric facts in REDengine and Witcher 3, relational "databases" of facts plus goal lifecycles in Larian's Osiris, quest aliases plus an event-driven Story Manager in Bethesda's Creation Kit. Content is organized as graphs (quest phases, scenes, dialogue trees) that read and write those facts. Scale reaches 1–2M words, 10^5 voice files and thousands of interlinked variables. Authoring tools such as articy:draft hit performance limits, and Larian said it did not even track its own dialogue volume.

### Cited Findings
- **Witcher 3 / REDengine Facts DB:** "a base of variables with number values assigned to them". Facts are set by scripts or by the quest block "FactsDB Change" and "later checked in quest, scene or script conditions". In the REDkit Quest Debugger, facts show a value ("Val:") and can be set to expire ("Exp. no" marks non-expiring facts). The debugger shows the quest tree "from the last loaded save" with "currently active signals highlighted" in the node graph. — [Witcher 3 REDkit: Quests Debugger (P)](https://cdprojektred.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/W3REDkit/pages/6328642/TOOL:+Quests+Debbuger); [REDkit: quest nodes (P)](https://cdprojektred.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/W3REDkit/pages/6327742/HOW-TO:+Use+quest+nodes); [Witcher 2 REDkit facts (P)](https://redkitwiki.cdprojektred.com/facts.htm)
- **Cyberpunk 2077 / REDengine 4:** quests are files referencing *questphases* and *scenes*. Questphases are graphs connected through handle refs and act as "high-level orchestrators that handle spawning NPCs, managing world states, updating journal entries, triggering rewards". — [redmodding wiki: Visualize quest files](https://wiki.redmodding.org/cyberpunk-2077-modding/modding-guides/quest/visualize-quest-files); [Building WolvenKit's Quest and Scene Editor](https://www.misterchedda.com/blog/wkit-scene-quest-editor) (community-reverse-engineered, S)
- CDPR's GDC talk "10 Key Quest Design Lessons from The Witcher 3 and Cyberpunk 2077" (Paweł Sasko) covers structuring choices and consequences in non-linear stories. — [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1028897/10-Key-Quest-Design-Lessons) (talk content not retrieved)
- **Baldur's Gate 3 / Larian Osiris:** "Osiris' responsibility is to handle the global world and game states, and the transitions between these states." A *story* is made of *goals*, each with INIT / KB (rules) / EXIT sections. On `GoalCompleted`, EXIT runs and the goal's rules deactivate, but "Databases defined in the goal will still be active after completing the goal." Adding a DB row "is often referred to as defining a fact". Goals are merged and "sorted alphabetically" for rule execution order. — [BG3 Modding Docs: Introduction to Osiris (P)](https://docs.baldursgate3.game/Scripting:_Introduction_to_Osiris)
- **BG3 scale:** fans extracted **173,642 voice files** across ~2,068 characters, totalling **236 h 55 m** of spoken dialogue. — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/baldurs-gate/baldurs-gate-3-fan-discovers-the-game-has-nearly-237-hours-of-spoken-dialogue-in-it-and-a-whopping-14-hours-are-narrator-amelia-tylers-alone-though-astarion-gives-her-a-run-for-her-money/). Swen Vincke said Larian "weren't really keeping track" of the amount. — [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/baldurs-gate/after-baldurs-gate-3-player-painstakingly-calculates-how-much-spoken-dialogue-larian-included-in-its-d-and-d-rpg-director-admits-they-didnt-actually-know-themselves-which-i-think-was-for-the-better/). Larian's pre-launch claims: **174 hours of cinematics** ([TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/baldurs-gate-3-174-hours-of-cinematics-twice-length-game-of-thrones/)) and **~17,000 ending variations** arising from interlinked variables ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/baldurs-gate-3-has-17000-variations-on-its-ending-a-number-that-gives-me-a-headache-just-thinking-about-it/)). **Word-count conflict:** "2 million words" in press summaries vs ~1.365–1.5M words of dialogue in fan counts ([GamesRadar "triple LOTR"](https://www.gamesradar.com/baldurs-gate-3-has-triple-the-word-count-of-lord-of-the-rings-and-its-cutscenes-are-twice-as-long-as-game-of-thrones/), snippet).
- **Disco Elysium:** the script is "over 1 million words". — [GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/disco-elysiums-script-is-over-a-million-words-long). The narrator alone recorded ~350,000 words. — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/we-talk-to-disco-elysiums-incredible-narrator-who-recorded-350000-words-of-dialogue-and-has-never-acted-before/). The game was authored in articy:draft, and the state discipline was: "Whenever there's a memorable moment, tag it with a Boolean. This makes creating callbacks and personalized content easy" (Cash DeCuir), plus "at least one [document] for every in-game task". — [articy showcase (P)](https://www.articy.com/en/showcase/disco-elysium/). The dialogue volume made articy "janky" and at one point it "froze completely, because it definitely wasn't built for it". — [PC Gamer, "had so much text it broke the branching narrative software"](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/rpg/disco-elysium-had-so-much-text-it-broke-the-branching-narrative-software-we-were-writing-too-much/) (snippet; page did not render)
- **Skyrim Radiant Story:** quests use **Aliases**, references or locations filled at runtime (fixed, random from a list, created from a leveled list, or taken from another quest), so "any actor can 'step into the role'". The **Story Manager** receives game events (actor death, entering a location, level-up) at SM Event Nodes, which are decision trees that start quests. — [Creation Kit Wiki: Quest Alias Tab](https://ck.uesp.net/wiki/Quest_Alias_Tab); [Category: Story Manager](https://ck.uesp.net/wiki/Category:Story_Manager); [Category: Radiant Story](https://ck.uesp.net/wiki/Category:Radiant_Story) (P-ish, community-maintained official-docs mirror)
- **80 Days (inkle, ink):** the free 2015 "Season 2" update added 30+ cities (169 total) and two endings, taking the script to ~**750,000 words**. — [inkle blog (P)](https://www.inklestudios.com/2015/09/17/new_adventures.html); [TouchArcade](https://toucharcade.com/2015/09/30/the-fantastic-80-days-just-got-a-huge-content-update/)

### Inferences
- **The fact DB is the common denominator.** It ranges from flat numeric facts with expiry (REDengine) to relational tables with rule-based triggers (Osiris, essentially a Datalog-like production system) to role bindings (Skyrim aliases). Narrata's storage abstraction should support at least: typed scalar facts, **relational facts (tuples) with indexed queries**, **fact expiry/time-bounded validity**, and **parametric role binding** (Starfreighter/Skyrim style). A plain `HashMap<String, Value>` variable store, which is what ink and Yarn default to, is insufficient at AAA scale.
- **Lifecycle-scoped rules with persistent facts** (Osiris: rules die with the goal, DBs survive) map naturally onto Narrata's statecharts: a region's transitions are active only while the state is entered, while facts it wrote persist. This separation also makes migration easier, because dead rules carry no state.
- **Event-driven activation (Story Manager) versus polling storylets:** at 10^4+ guarded units, selection must be indexed. Re-evaluate only the guards whose input facts changed (dependency tracking or an incremental Rete-style match), not every storylet each tick. This is an inference, but it is implied by the scale numbers and by Kreminski's incremental sifting work ([Winnow, AIIDE 2021](https://mkremins.github.io/publications/Winnow_AIIDE2021.pdf)).
- **Tooling must scale to roughly 10^6 words and 10^5 lines.** Disco Elysium's articy freezes and Larian not tracking dialogue volume argue for engine-side indexing and metrics: content counts, fact read/write graphs, per-package stats. They also argue for authoring formats that diff and merge as text, rather than one monolithic project file.
- **Debugger requirements:** CDPR's quest debugger (live fact values, active graph signals, load from save) is the baseline. Narrata's time-travel store can go further by showing *when and by which node* each fact changed.

### Gaps
- No public counts of facts, flags or variables for Witcher 3, Cyberpunk, BG3 or Disco Elysium; only word, voice and ending counts.
- Pentiment, Pillars of Eternity and Larian's internal story tooling (Story Editor scale, compile times): not found.
- BG3 patch-to-patch save compatibility handling in Osiris (how new goals are injected into existing saves): no primary source found. The Osiris docs say nothing about savegames.

## Q3. Web serials, story bibles and franchise canon databases: what do they imply for a canonical narrative state store?

### Takeaway
The most-cited industrial continuity system is Lucasfilm's **Holocron**, a FileMaker database (started January 2000) with tens of thousands of entries. Each entry carries a **canon-level field (G/T/C/S/N/D)** that encodes *authority and precedence*. The 2014 "Legends" reset showed that canon can be re-tiered wholesale. This argues for a canonical store whose facts carry provenance and authority tiers, and which can re-classify instead of delete.

### Cited Findings
- The Holocron continuity database was "created in January 2000" and "maintained by Leland Chee on FileMaker Pro". It "included a field for a single letter (G, T, C, S, N or D) representing the level of canonicity". G = George Lucas and the films, T = television, C = continuity (Expanded Universe) canon "unless superseded by G- or T-canon", S = secondary, N = non-canon. — [Wookieepedia: Holocron continuity database](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Holocron_continuity_database) (S, fan wiki)
- Chee reportedly said the Holocron was "up to nearly **80,000 entries** … 80,000 different characters, vehicles, planets, locations", up from an earlier ">30,000". The Lucasfilm Story Group (founded 2013) "determines all Star Wars canon". — search-result summaries of [Indiana Jones Wiki: Leland Chee](https://indianajones.fandom.com/wiki/Leland_Chee), [ComicBook.com](https://comicbook.com/starwars/news/star-wars-canon-lucasfilm-story-group-leland-chee-chewbacca-death/) (snippet). Chee also maintains an "Indycron" for Indiana Jones and "LFLcron" for other franchises (same snippet). ComicBook.com's headline attributes the decision to "erase most Star Wars stories" (the 2014 Legends reset) partly to the difficulty of continuing around Chewbacca's death.
- Fallen London's fan wiki reached about **25,000 pages**. The community, not the developer, became the de facto canon index for a 15-year serial. — [IF50](https://if50.substack.com/p/2009-fallen-london)

### Inferences
- **Provenance and authority as first-class fact metadata.** A canonical store for multi-author, multi-year narrative (shared universes, UGC packs, LLM-generated content) needs each fact tagged with *source package / author / authority tier*. Conflicts are resolved by explicit precedence, Holocron-style "C unless superseded by G/T", rather than last-write-wins.
- **Re-tiering instead of deletion.** The Legends reset is a bulk *reclassification*. Paired with Destiny's vault (Q1) and Zep's invalidate-don't-delete (Q4), this is a consistent pattern: a narrative store should support marking facts and content non-canonical or archived while keeping them queryable for history, time travel and fan reference.
- **Entities matter, not just variables.** Holocron entries are entities (characters, vehicles, planets). A long-narrative store benefits from an entity/relationship layer (who, where, what) above raw flags, which also matches the LLM-memory findings in Q4.

### Gaps
- Story-bible tools (World Anvil, Campfire, Plottr, Scrivener) and web-serial continuity practice (e.g., *Worm*, royalroad serials) were not researched within budget. Their data models are unknown here.
- The Holocron's actual schema (fields beyond canon level, how timeline and dates are modeled) is not public in any source found.

## Q4. LLM-era dynamic narrative (2023–2026): how do AI storytelling platforms and agent-memory systems store state, and what are the lessons?

### Takeaway
The field moved through four stages. (1) Raw context. (2) Keyword-triggered lore injection: AI Dungeon Story Cards, NovelAI Lorebook, SillyTavern World Info, and the `character_book` in chara_card_v2/v3. (3) Embedding-retrieved summaries: AI Dungeon Memory Bank, Generative Agents' memory stream, MemGPT tiers. (4) **Structured or temporal state as source of truth**: Hidden Door's card-based world DB, Latitude Voyage's "deterministic World Engine", Zep/Graphiti's bi-temporal knowledge graph, Mem0's ADD/UPDATE/DELETE consolidation. 2026 benchmarks show LLMs and memory systems still fail badly at tracking *superseded* facts over long horizons. The engine, not the model, must own canonical state.

### Cited Findings

**Prompt-injection lore systems**
- AI Dungeon Memory System: a memory is created by summarizing **every 6 actions**, starting at action 12. Auto-summarization of the overall plot runs **every 15 actions**. Memory Bank caps by tier: **25 / 100 / 200 / 400** memories (Free / Champion / Legend / Mythic). Retrieval is by embedding relevance. Story Cards are keyword-triggered and independent of the memory system. — [AI Dungeon Help: The Memory System (P)](https://help.aidungeon.com/faq/the-memory-system)
- NovelAI Lorebook: entries activate when a key appears in the recent-context search window. Keys wrapped in `/…/` are regex, and `&` requires multiple keys. Context is assembled in budgeted "stages" by insertion order. — [NovelAI Docs: Lorebook (P)](https://docs.novelai.net/en/text/lorebook/); [Advanced Settings (P)](https://docs.novelai.net/en/text/editor/advancedsettings/)
- Character Card V2 (`spec: "chara_card_v2"`, `spec_version: "2.0"`): the optional `character_book` has `scan_depth`, `token_budget`, `recursive_scanning`, `extensions`, and `entries[]`. Each entry has `keys`, `content`, `extensions`, `enabled`, `insertion_order` (plus optional `case_sensitive`, `name`, `priority`, `id`, `comment`, `selective`, `secondary_keys`, `constant`, `position`). Interop rule: "Character editors **MUST NOT** destroy unknown key-value pairs when importing and exporting", and apps should namespace extensions (e.g., `"agnai/voice"`). — [character-card-spec-v2 (P)](https://github.com/malfoyslastname/character-card-spec-v2/blob/main/spec_v2.md). V3 (kwaroran) adds assets and richer lorebook features in a separate `ccv3` PNG chunk. — [aituts/honeychat summaries](https://honeychat.bot/en/blog/ai-character-cards-guide-v2-v3-2026/) (S; one summary dated V3 to "~May 2026", which conflicts with V3's earlier community adoption. Verify at [github.com/kwaroran/character-card-spec-v3](https://github.com/kwaroran/character-card-spec-v3), not fetched).

**Agent-memory architectures**
- Generative Agents (Park et al., Apr 2023): store "a complete record of the agent's experiences using natural language", "synthesize those memories over time into higher-level reflections", and "retrieve them dynamically to plan behavior". Ablations show observation, planning and reflection "each contribute critically". — [arXiv 2304.03442 (P)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03442). Retrieval scores memories by recency (exponential decay) × importance (LLM-rated integer) × relevance (embedding cosine). — [summary, AgentPatterns](https://agentpatterns.ai/agent-design/generative-agents-memory-stream/) (S)
- MemGPT (Packer et al., Oct 2023): OS-style "virtual context management". Core (in-context) memory, *recall storage* (the full event history) and *archival storage* (a read-write overflow DB) are paged by function calls, like page faults. — [arXiv 2310.08560 (P)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08560)
- **Zep / Graphiti (Jan 2025):** three subgraph tiers. *Episodes* hold "raw input data in the form of messages, text, or JSON". *Entities* are "extracted from episodes and resolved with existing graph entities". *Communities* are clusters. It is **bi-temporal**: "Timeline T represents the chronological ordering of events, and timeline T′ represents the transactional order of Zep's data ingestion". Edges carry `t_valid`/`t_invalid` ∈ T and `t'_created`/`t'_expired` ∈ T′. On contradiction "it invalidates the affected edges by setting their t_invalid to the t_valid of the invalidating edge", which is invalidation rather than deletion. Retrieval combines cosine, BM25 and BFS n-hop search with RRF/MMR/cross-encoder reranking. Results: DMR 94.8% vs MemGPT 93.4%. LongMemEval (gpt-4o) 71.2% vs 60.2% baseline, latency 2.58 s vs 28.9 s, context **1.6k vs 115k tokens**. — [arXiv 2501.13956 (P)](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.13956)
- **Mem0 (Apr 2025):** an extract → update pipeline in which an LLM tool-call chooses ADD / UPDATE / DELETE / NOOP against similar existing memories. The graph variant Mem0g stores entity-relation triplets with conflict detection. Reported "26% relative improvements" over OpenAI memory on LOCOMO, "91% lower p95 latency", ">90% token cost" savings. The graph memory adds "around 2% higher overall score". — [arXiv 2504.19413 (P)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.19413)

**Structured-engine narrative platforms**
- Hidden Door: "We update our game engine layer with a structured representation of everything in your world, from story events to character stats and conditions." "Our narrative team of real humans write flexible, structured plot beats, and we then use a variety of ML tools to combine those beats." Each turn runs "a bunch of different tasks, some of them purely programmatic, some using … 'classic' machine learning, a few using LLMs." For licensed IP, authors decide "which moment the player characters will enter their worlds". — [Hidden Door FAQ (P)](https://www.hiddendoor.co/help/faq). Coverage describes the state as cards (characters, items, locations, story beats). — [Arcanum review](https://arcanumrpgs.com/blog/hidden-door-review/) (S)
- Latitude **Voyage** (launched 21 Apr 2026; opened to all later in 2026): powered by a "proprietary World Engine, a deterministic system that governs world state, character memory, and rule enforcement". It tracks "health, inventory, currency, geography, relationships and long-term consequences across thousands of turns so the AI can't hallucinate them away". Latitude cites "five years of R&D and six prototype engines", 160,000+ AI characters met by testers, and ~3,000 choices per average player. — [TechCrunch (21 Apr 2026)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/21/voyage-is-an-ai-rpg-platform-for-creating-custom-gaming-worlds-with-ai-generated-npc-interactions/); [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/latitude-opens-voyage-to-everyone-introducing-the-second-generation-of-ai-roleplay-302860961.html) (snippet; Latitude's page did not render)

**Long-horizon consistency evidence (2026)**
- *Lost in Stories / ConStory-Bench* (Mar 2026; ACL Findings 2026): 2,000 prompts, a taxonomy of 5 error categories and 19 subtypes. Errors are "most common in factual and temporal dimensions", "tend to appear around the middle of narratives", and occur in higher-entropy segments. — [arXiv 2603.05890 (P)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05890); [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.410/)
- *NCP-Bench* ("Can LLM Agents Stick to the Script?", Aug 2026, ICML 2026): 100 interactive environments, each with a "structured narrative specification (trajectory, commitments, and initial facts)". GPT-5.2 achieved a "42% survival rate after 20 turns". Fact-conflict rates were "40% to 68% across models". Few runs satisfied all commitments within 100 turns. — [arXiv 2608.08160 (P)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08160)
- *StateMemBench* ("Can Agent Memory Systems Track Evolving State?", Aug 2026): 234 multi-session scenarios. Existing memory systems scored **0.149–0.205 current-state accuracy**. The state-first wrapper StateMem, which tracks supersession and dependencies explicitly, reached 0.363 and gave +32 to +67 points across six backends. Core failure: systems "struggle to distinguish current versus superseded facts". — [arXiv 2608.19652 (P)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19652)

### Inferences
- **The engine owns truth; LLMs propose deltas.** The 2026 benchmarks (42% survival at 20 turns, 40–68% fact conflicts, ≤0.205 current-state accuracy) plus the commercial pivot to deterministic engines (Hidden Door, Voyage) point the same way. For "continuously generated" narrative, Narrata should accept LLM output only as **typed, validated transactions** against the fact store (schema-checked, guard-checked, provenance-tagged). Narration is a *view* over facts.
- **Bi-temporal facts fit time-travel saves directly.** Zep's `t_valid/t_invalid` (story time) and `t'_created/t'_expired` (system/ingestion time) correspond to Narrata's *story-time history* and *program/content-version history*. Two concrete uses: (a) "what did the world believe at story time t?" for flashbacks, rewinds and branch comparison; (b) "what did version v of the content assert?" for auditing migrations and hot reloads. Supersession should be explicit (invalidate, don't delete), which is the exact property StateMem shows is missing elsewhere.
- **Lorebook semantics belong in a retrieval layer, not the core.** Keyword- and regex-triggered entries with token budgets, insertion order and recursion are a *context-assembly policy*. Narrata can expose a pluggable "context projection" API (facts → prompt) while keeping storage structured. The V2 rule "MUST NOT destroy unknown key-value pairs" plus namespaced extensions is a good interop rule for Narrata's package and save formats too (forward compatibility with unknown fields).
- **Bounded memory needs summarization or compaction tiers.** AI Dungeon's 6-action memories, 15-action summaries and tier caps, along with MemGPT's paging, show that unbounded per-player history is not kept in hot storage. For multi-year saves Narrata's time-travel store needs **snapshots + compaction + cold archival** of the event log, with derived summaries (facts) kept hot.
- **Graph vs vector vs structured:** production systems use hybrids. Zep combines cosine, BM25 and graph BFS. Mem0's graph variant adds about 2%, while structured engines (Hidden Door, Voyage) target correctness. For a narrative engine, canonical **structured/relational facts** should be primary, with **vector indexes as secondary, rebuildable projections** for fuzzy recall.

### Gaps
- Character.AI memory, Inworld's memory/knowledge architecture, and Latitude Voyage's World Engine internals (data model, storage backend): no primary technical documentation found.
- No published head-to-head evaluation of graph vs vector vs structured state *for interactive fiction specifically*. NCP-Bench's abstract does not report which state techniques help.

## Q5. Hot reload and live authoring during play: how do runtime state and changed content reconcile?

### Takeaway
Mainstream narrative middleware does **not** robustly migrate an in-flight execution position across content edits. ink's save state is fragile to structural edits because choices and gathers get *incremental, positional* names. Yarn Spinner saves only variables plus the current node and cannot restore mid-node. The robust live-authoring trick is **replay**: Inky remembers the player's choices and, on recompile, "fast-forwards to the last point you were at". Inform's Skein replays branches against "blessed" transcripts for regression testing.

### Cited Findings
- ink v0.8.0/0.8.1 (Apr 2019): "Big improvements in how ink can cope with saving/loading a save state when the ink story has been changed." "If a variable is added, it will no longer crash … It will try to use the original declared value." `ChoosePathString` on a path that no longer exists will "do its best to work its way up the path and find content that _does_ exist." Caveat: perfect compatibility is impossible, so "always exercise caution, especially when editing content for a game that's in production." — [ink releases (P)](https://github.com/inkle/ink/releases)
- Root cause of ink save fragility: ink assigns locations to choices and gathers *incrementally*, so "adding or removing a choice or gather will put it out of sync with your old save state". — [inky issue #253](https://github.com/inkle/inky/issues/253); [ink issue #572](https://github.com/inkle/ink/issues/572) (P, issue tracker)
- Inky: "The play pane remembers the choices that you made, so when Inky recompiles, it fast-forwards to the last point you were at." — [Inky README (P)](https://github.com/inkle/inky)
- ink Unity integration auto-recompiles .ink to JSON on save, but by default "ink does not compile while in play mode" (configurable). The Ink Player window supports editing variables on the fly, save/load of states, and divert. — [ink-unity-integration: InkPlayerWindow (P)](https://github.com/inkle/ink-unity-integration/blob/master/Documentation/InkPlayerWindow.md)
- Yarn Spinner: "It is not currently possible to save or restore the specific line that the dialogue is running". You save `DialogueRunner.CurrentNode` and the variables (`SaveStateToPersistentStorage`), then restart at the node start. A community gist restores a raw program counter. — [Yarn Spinner FAQ (P)](https://docs.yarnspinner.dev/2.3/using-yarnspinner-with-unity/faq); [gist](https://gist.github.com/Starviling/71dc1fc204b22d5a1d288cddc84449bb). Variable storage is a pluggable abstract class (`VariableStorageBehaviour`, with `InMemoryVariableStorage` as default). — [Yarn Spinner: Variable Storage (P)](https://docs.yarnspinner.dev/2.3/guides/yarn-variables-and-variable-storage)
- Inform 7: `Test … with "…"` scripts and the Skein. Responses can be marked "blessed", and on replay "variations from the blessed version will be highlighted". "Replay Commands Blessed in Transcript" replays all skein threads containing a blessed knot and stops at the first mismatch. — [Inform 7 docs §2.8 (P)](http://inform7.com/book/WI_2_8.html); [Inform release notes 7-2 (P)](https://github.com/ganelson/inform/blob/master/notes/release/7-2.md)
- FFXIV's live-content edits (Q1) followed the same "reset position to a safe point" approach at MMO scale: abandon, re-accept, or skip to the next quest. — [Patch 5.3 notes (P)](https://na.finalfantasyxiv.com/lodestone/topics/detail/c2f8c5ba14d9cfaa09d5aab63c5c2da260eae21a)

### Inferences
- **Stable, author-assigned IDs are mandatory.** ink's breakage comes directly from positional or incremental addressing. Narrata nodes, choices, gathers and statechart states should carry **stable IDs** (UUID or author label plus package namespace) that survive reordering. Renames should go through explicit **redirect/tombstone** records consumed by migrations.
- **Two complementary reconciliation strategies:**
  1. **Replay (event-sourcing)**: log player inputs (choice IDs, not indices) plus RNG seeds. On a new program version, deterministically re-execute and detect the first divergence, as Inky and the Inform Skein do. Narrata's deterministic runtime makes this cheap and exact.
  2. **Fact migration + safe-point re-entry**: when replay diverges or the history is too long, carry over the fact store (with defaults for new variables, as in ink 0.8) and re-enter at the nearest valid safe point (ink's "work up the path" or Yarn's node start).
- **Declare safe points.** Yarn's node-boundary restore and FFXIV's quest-boundary reset suggest letting authors mark **checkpoint-safe states**: no in-flight locals, so the position is re-derivable from facts. Allow saves anywhere, but guarantee migration only at safe points.
- **Divergence oracle.** Inform's blessed transcripts are a cheap regression oracle. Narrata should store golden playthroughs (input log plus expected fact-diffs and outputs) per package and run them on every content change. Save-compat tests ("load old saves with new story", which ink recommends) should run in CI.

### Gaps
- articy:draft live sync / runtime update behavior, and Yarn Spinner hot reload during play mode: no primary documentation found.
- No published postmortem on how a shipped game reconciled *hot-patched* dialogue with mid-conversation player state (all evidence covers editor-time reload or quest-boundary resets).

## Q6. Engineering patterns for scale: partitioning, lazy loading, verification and reachability checking

### Takeaway
At scale, content is partitioned by **location, quest or goal, and package**. Storylets are mostly location-bound, Osiris uses goals, CDPR uses questphases, Destiny uses destinations as its unit of vaulting. Verification tooling exists in research and partly in practice: ASP-queried playtrace spaces (DendryScope), invariant proofs (Ceptre), formal reachability on industrial-size narratives ((P)NFG), bounded model checking of IF, incremental story sifting (Winnow/Felt), and transcript regression (Inform). QBN's implicit links are exactly where reachability bugs hide.

### Cited Findings
- **Partitioning in practice:** StoryNexus storylets are "nearly all … location-bound" ([Kreminski & Wardrip-Fruin (P)](https://mkremins.github.io/publications/Storylets_SketchingAMap.pdf)). Osiris stories are composed of goals with activation lifecycles ([BG3 Modding Docs (P)](https://docs.baldursgate3.game/Scripting:_Introduction_to_Osiris)). Cyberpunk quests decompose into questphases and scenes ([redmodding wiki](https://wiki.redmodding.org/cyberpunk-2077-modding/modding-guides/quest/visualize-quest-files)). Destiny vaulted by destination and campaign ([DestinyTracker](https://destinytracker.com/destiny-2/articles/bungie-plans-vault-destiny-2-content-year-4-beyond)).
- **DendryScope (AIIDE 2023):** QBNs "are hypertexts with extensive implicit linked structure, where the observation of one passage can have non-obvious long-range implications for the reachability of other passages". The tool visually summarizes all possible playtraces and uses **answer set programming** as a query language over playtraces. It works on QBNs written in Dendry. — [AAAI/AIIDE (P)](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/27527); [camera-ready PDF](https://jazztap.github.io/dendry-paper/2023_AIIDE_Dendryscope_Camera_Ready.pdf)
- **Ceptre (Martens, AIIDE 2015):** a linear-logic language for generative interactive systems, with "techniques for stating and proving program invariants, as well as a decidability proof for automatically checking those invariants for a large fragment of the language". — [Ceptre paper (P)](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~cmartens/ceptre.pdf); [thesis CMU-CS-15-134 (P)](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~cmartens/thesis/thesis.pdf)
- **(P)NFG:** shows "formal verification of a wide variety of properties can be feasibly performed on non-trivial, industrial-size game narratives through reachability analyses", including temporal properties up to the degree of "pointlessness". — [(P)NFG paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/261472065_PNFG_A_Language_and_Runtime_System_for_Structured_Computer_Narratives) (snippet)
- **Bounded model checking of IF:** insert an assertion that is violated on completion, and the model checker's counterexample is a walkthrough. Demonstrated on ~1980 IF games. — [arXiv 2012.15365 (P)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.15365)
- **Story sifting:** *Felt* is a 290-line JS sifter backed by DataScript (a Datalog DB) ([Felt, ICIDS 2019 (P)](https://mkremins.github.io/publications/Felt_SimpleStorySifter.pdf)). *Winnow* is a DSL for **incremental** sifting that detects storyful event sequences "while they are still playing out" ([Winnow, AIIDE 2021 (P)](https://mkremins.github.io/publications/Winnow_AIIDE2021.pdf)). Kreminski & Wang used ASP to guide and diversify LLM story generation (Wordplay @ ACL 2024). — [Kreminski CV](https://mkremins.github.io/cv/) (snippet)
- **Transcript regression:** Inform's blessed Skein replay ([Inform 7 docs (P)](http://inform7.com/book/WI_2_8.html)). The ink team advises testing old saves against new story versions ([ink releases (P)](https://github.com/inkle/ink/releases)).
- **Size pressure is real:** Destiny 2 exceeded 100 GB, which motivated vaulting ([DestinyTracker](https://destinytracker.com/destiny-2/articles/bungie-plans-vault-destiny-2-content-year-4-beyond)). Fallen London adds "many hundreds of storylets" per year ([IF50](https://if50.substack.com/p/2009-fallen-london)).

### Inferences
- **Package = unit of partitioning, loading, versioning and archiving.** Narrata's composable node packages should be the granularity for lazy loading and streaming (load a package when its guard or location becomes reachable), for content-addressed versioning, and for archive/unarchive (Destiny-style vaulting without deleting player facts). Each package declares which facts it reads and writes, so dependency and impact analysis is possible.
- **Static analysis over declared fact I/O.** With per-package fact read/write sets, Narrata can compute: unreachable nodes (guards over facts nobody writes), dead writes (facts nobody reads), cross-package coupling, and "facts that old saves lack", which feed migration defaults. This is the cheap, always-on layer.
- **Deeper verification as an optional backend.** Export the flow/statechart + guard semantics to ASP (clingo) or a model checker for bounded reachability ("can ending E be reached without fact F?"), as DendryScope, Ceptre and (P)NFG do. Determinism and explicit fact schemas make this export tractable. Run it offline, not in the reader.
- **Incremental guard evaluation.** Winnow-style incremental matching, or a Datalog/Rete-style fact engine (cf. Felt on DataScript, Osiris rules), is the scalable way to select among 10^4+ storylets. Re-evaluate only guards whose input facts changed.

### Gaps
- No public evidence that a major studio (CDPR, Larian, Bethesda, Failbetter) uses formal model checking in production. Industry practice appears to be debuggers plus QA plus transcript-style regression.
- Nelson & Mateas' drama-management search work and Yarn Spinner's static-analysis features were not retrieved within budget.

## Q7. Synthesis: what ultra-long and live narratives demand of Narrata's core and storage

### Takeaway
The evidence supports Narrata's thesis (abstract logic + pluggable storage), with a specific shape. The **durable, versioned, provenance-tagged fact store** is the product. Execution positions are ephemeral and re-derivable. Content is **additive, stably-addressed packages** that can be archived but never orphan facts. Evolution runs through **replay + migration + safe-point re-entry**, and correctness at scale needs **static and formal analysis over declared fact dependencies**. LLM-generated content must enter as validated transactions against this store, preferably bi-temporal.

### Cited Findings
- Facts-not-positions across versions and titles: Telltale's 42-start-point story generator ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/the-walking-dead-season-three/the-walking-dead-season-3-save-game-transfers)), ME3's 1,000+ imported variables ([Game Rant](https://gamerant.com/mass-effect-3-1000-variables/)), Dragon Age Keep's 300+ decisions ([DA Wiki](https://dragonage.fandom.com/wiki/Dragon_Age_Keep)), FFXIV's abandon/re-accept/skip rules ([Patch 5.3 (P)](https://na.finalfantasyxiv.com/lodestone/topics/detail/c2f8c5ba14d9cfaa09d5aab63c5c2da260eae21a)), Yarn's variables + node-start restore ([Yarn FAQ (P)](https://docs.yarnspinner.dev/2.3/using-yarnspinner-with-unity/faq)).
- Positional addressing breaks saves: ink's incremental choice/gather naming ([inky #253](https://github.com/inkle/inky/issues/253)). Mitigations are defaults for new variables and path fallback ([ink releases (P)](https://github.com/inkle/ink/releases)).
- Replay as live-authoring reconciliation: Inky fast-forward ([Inky README (P)](https://github.com/inkle/inky)) and Inform's blessed-transcript replay ([Inform docs (P)](http://inform7.com/book/WI_2_8.html)).
- Invalidate-don't-delete and bi-temporality: Zep/Graphiti ([arXiv 2501.13956 (P)](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.13956)). Canon tiers and re-tiering: Holocron ([Wookieepedia](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Holocron_continuity_database)). Content removal harms players: Destiny vault and its 2026 reversal ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/destiny-2/campaign-removed); [GamerHub](https://gamerhub.co.uk/bungie-destiny-2-unvaulting-red-war/)).
- Scale envelope: 4.5M words over 15+ years ([Failbetter (P)](https://www.failbettergames.com/news/fallen-londons-15th-birthday)), ~1–2M words and 173,642 voice files in BG3 ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/baldurs-gate/baldurs-gate-3-fan-discovers-the-game-has-nearly-237-hours-of-spoken-dialogue-in-it-and-a-whopping-14-hours-are-narrator-amelia-tylers-alone-though-astarion-gives-her-a-run-for-her-money/)), >1M words in Disco Elysium ([GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/disco-elysiums-script-is-over-a-million-words-long)), ~80k canon entities in the Holocron (snippet), thousands of turns in AI RPGs ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/21/voyage-is-an-ai-rpg-platform-for-creating-custom-gaming-worlds-with-ai-generated-npc-interactions/)).
- LLM state unreliability: NCP-Bench 42% survival at 20 turns and 40–68% fact conflicts ([arXiv 2608.08160](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08160)). StateMemBench ≤0.205 current-state accuracy for existing memory systems ([arXiv 2608.19652](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19652)). Structured engines as the industry response ([Hidden Door FAQ (P)](https://www.hiddendoor.co/help/faq)).

### Inferences: concrete requirements for Narrata

**Core (runtime/VM)**
1. **Fact/position separation as an architectural invariant.** The flow VM and statecharts hold *ephemeral* positions (call stack, active states, locals). All durable meaning lives in the fact store. Authors mark **safe points** where position is fully re-derivable from facts. Migration is guaranteed only from safe points.
2. **Stable addressing.** Every node, choice, state, storylet and fact key gets a stable ID namespaced by package (`pkg@major/...`). Renames and removals produce **redirect/tombstone** entries. Never use positional indices in saves. Choice logs record choice IDs.
3. **Deterministic replay as a first-class operation.** Persist an input log (choice IDs, external events, RNG seeds, LLM outputs as recorded values). The same log against program version v' re-executes and reports the first divergence (Inky/Skein pattern). This powers hot reload, save migration validation and regression tests.
4. **Indexed, incremental guard evaluation** for storylet/salience selection over 10^4+ units (Winnow/Rete-style), with per-package fact read/write declarations.
5. **Compensating migration primitives**: re-enter at the nearest safe point or ancestor path (ink 0.8 / FFXIV), default-initialize new facts (ink 0.8), grant or refund facts (Cyberpunk 2.0), and show player-visible notices. Migrations are chained per version (FFXIV's 4.0 and 4.4 legacy cases).

**Storage (pluggable backends)**
6. **Typed fact store with richer than key→value semantics**: scalars, relational tuples (Osiris DBs), entity/relationship records (Holocron, Zep entities), role bindings (Skyrim aliases), and optional **expiry** (REDengine facts).
7. **Bi-temporal / versioned facts**: valid-time (story time, for time-travel and branch views) and transaction-time (when recorded, which content version). Contradictions **invalidate** rather than delete (Zep). This merges naturally with the time-travel save store: a save is a pointer into this history, not a blob.
8. **Provenance and authority tiers on facts and content** (package, author, human vs LLM, canon tier à la Holocron G/T/C/S/N), with explicit precedence rules for conflicts and support for bulk re-tiering ("Legends").
9. **History growth control**: snapshots + log compaction + cold archival for multi-year saves. Derived summary facts stay hot (cf. AI Dungeon's summary cadence and tiered memory caps). Secondary indexes (vector, full-text) are **rebuildable projections**, never the source of truth.
10. **Content-addressed package storage with archive/restore**: unload by package (Destiny's lesson) without orphaning facts. Keep old versions retrievable so old saves can replay against the exact content they were made with, and so migrations can be audited.
11. **Cross-package / cross-season export manifests**: each package declares exportable facts with defaults and an optional questionnaire generator (Telltale story generator, ME Genesis, LiS dialogue restatement), small and versioned (Veilguard cut 300+ inputs to about 3).
12. **Forward-compatible serialization**: unknown fields and namespaced extensions must round-trip unchanged (the chara_card_v2 "MUST NOT destroy unknown key-value pairs" rule), so older readers (e.g., the Wasm reader) don't corrupt newer saves.

**Tooling and verification**
13. Always-on static analysis from declared fact I/O: unreachable content, dead writes, and new-fact defaults required by old saves.
14. Optional formal backends (ASP/clingo or model checking) for bounded reachability and invariant queries (DendryScope, Ceptre, (P)NFG).
15. Golden-playthrough regression (Inform's blessed transcripts) and "old saves × new content" compatibility tests in CI (ink's guidance).
16. A REDkit-class debugger: live fact values with history (which node wrote what, when), active flow/statechart highlights, load-from-save. The time-travel store makes "why is this fact X?" answerable.

### Gaps
- No primary source quantifies per-save state size (number of facts per player) for any large RPG or live game, so storage sizing for "millions of words" content vs per-player state remains inferred: content scale is large, per-save fact counts plausibly 10^3–10^5. Treat as an unverified estimate.
- No primary evidence was found on multi-player shared narrative state *sharding* (e.g., how WoW stores per-character phase/quest state server-side). Only player-facing behavior is documented.
- Narrative A/B testing and server-streamed story-chunk delivery (Choices/Episode/AI platforms) remain undocumented in the sources found.
